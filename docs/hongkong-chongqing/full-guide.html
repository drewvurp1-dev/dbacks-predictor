▼📋 Before you go — China entry (the big one), why the trip is shaped this way, group logistics
⚠️ Mainland China entry — read this now, not in December. Hong Kong and Macao are visa-free for US passports (90 / 30 days). The mainland is not. This trip enters mainland China on the 240-hour visa-free transit scheme: you board the bullet train at Hong Kong West Kowloon (an approved transit port since Nov 2025), clear Chinese immigration inside the station, and stay up to 10 days across Guangxi and Chongqing — both eligible regions. It works, but it has hard conditions and no margin for error with a group of nine. Tips → Entry Requirements has the full checklist and the zero-risk alternative (a regular 10-year tourist visa). Decide which route you're taking by early October — a visa takes weeks to arrange for nine people.
Why the trip is shaped this way: The coast days are front-loaded because the group converges in Hong Kong from two cities (seven from PHX, two from ORD) and everyone needs a day on the ground before a 6am start. Macao is a day trip, not a base — the only fast train inland leaves from Hong Kong, so you come back to Hong Kong to catch it. Guilin is a single night on purpose: it breaks a 7-hour rail day into two 4-hour ones and buys a river-town evening, without committing a day the group would rather spend in Chongqing. Chongqing gets five nights because it's the actual destination — New Year's Eve, the day trips, and enough slack to lose a morning without the plan falling over.
Group logistics: Nine people. Book train seats the moment the window opens (see The Rail Journey) — holiday-week second class sells out and a group split across two carriages is a headache with luggage. Restaurants for nine need calling ahead, especially in Hong Kong and on NYE. Assume two taxis / DiDis per hop, not one.

Airport Express → Kowloon / Hong Kong station📍 Map 24 minutes to Kowloon, HKD ~105 one way, trains every 10 min. Free porter and in-town check-in at the station. For nine people with luggage this beats taxis; a red taxi to TST is roughly HKD 270–350 if you'd rather. HKD 105 (~$13)
Dim sum lunch📍 Map First meal on the ground. Tim Ho Wan (Michelin, cheap, several branches), Lin Heung Kui (old-school trolley service on HK Island), or Din Tai Fung if the group wants a safe bet. Book ahead for nine. HKD 100–180 pp (~$13–23)
Victoria Peak📍 Map The view. Take the restored Peak Tram up (buy timed tickets online to skip the queue) or a taxi if the line is brutal. Walk the flat 3.5km Peak Circle Walk for the quieter panoramas away from the Sky Terrace crush. Go late afternoon so you catch daylight, sunset and the lights coming on. HKD 88–148 (~$11–19)
Star Ferry — Central to Tsim Sha Tsui📍 Map The best HKD 5 in Asia. Ten minutes across Victoria Harbour on a 100-year-old green-and-white ferry, skyline on both sides. Sit on the open lower deck. HKD 5 (~$0.65)
Symphony of Lights + TST promenade📍 Map Nightly 8:00pm laser-and-light show across the harbour towers, ~10 min, best viewed from the Avenue of Stars / promenade on the Kowloon side. Free. Wander the promenade afterward for the long-exposure skyline shot. Free
Temple Street Night Market📍 Map Yau Ma Tei. Stalls, fortune tellers, and open-air dai pai dong seafood joints — clay-pot rice, typhoon-shelter crab, cheap beer. Rowdy, touristy, fun. A relaxed first-night dinner that doesn't need a reservation. HKD 150–250 pp (~$19–32)

Sun · Dec 28
Macao — Portugal, then the Cotai Strip
Culture Food Night
MorningEarly ferry from Hong Kong; walk the Historic Centre — Senado Square to the Ruins of St. Paul's.
AfternoonTaipa Village for egg tarts and Macanese lunch; A-Ma Temple or Mount Fortress.
EveningCotai — the Venetian and Parisian, the light shows, back to Hong Kong on a late ferry.
Macao does 450 years of Portuguese colonial history and the biggest casino floors on earth within a few miles of each other. One full day covers the old town on foot and an evening on the Cotai Strip. It's a day trip — you sleep in Hong Kong tonight because tomorrow's train leaves from there.
⏱ If your booking actually has a Macao hotel night: the itinerary you gave me lists the 28th "in Macao." If that's an overnight, you still need to be back at Hong Kong West Kowloon by mid-morning on the 29th for the Guilin train — a first ferry (~07:00–07:30) plus the ride to West Kowloon is roughly 2 hours door to door. Tight but doable. Confirm which it is and adjust; this card assumes a same-day return.
Ferry: Hong Kong → Macao📍 Map TurboJET from Sheung Wan (Hong Kong–Macau Ferry Terminal, → Macao Outer Harbour) or Cotai Water Jet (→ Taipa, handy for the casinos). ~60–70 min, first sailings around 07:00–07:30 then roughly every 30–60 min. Bring passports — it's an international border both ways. Book the outbound the night before for a group of nine. HKD ~175 (~$22) each way
Senado Square & the Historic Centre📍 Map UNESCO-listed. Portuguese wave-pattern cobbles, pastel colonial facades, the Leal Senado building and St. Dominic's Church. Start here and follow the crowd uphill to St. Paul's. Free
Ruins of St. Paul's + Mount Fortress📍 Map The stone facade of a 17th-century Jesuit church — all that survived an 1835 fire — standing at the top of a grand staircase. Macao's signature image. Walk up to the adjacent Mount Fortress for cannons and a city view; the Macao Museum is built into it. Free
Taipa Village📍 Map Lunch. Narrow lanes of Macanese and Portuguese restaurants — order African chicken, minchi, bacalhau, a pork chop bun, and Lord Stow's or Margaret's Café e Nata for the original Portuguese egg tarts. This is the food stop of the day. MOP 150–300 pp (~$19–37)
A-Ma Temple📍 Map The oldest temple in Macao (1488) and the source of the name "Macao" itself. Incense-hazed halls climbing a hillside near the Inner Harbour. Skip if the group's flagging — it's a 15-min taxi from the old centre. Free
Cotai Strip — Venetian, Parisian, Londoner📍 Map The evening. Free casino shuttle buses connect every resort and both ferry terminals. Ride the Venetian's indoor gondolas, go up the half-scale Eiffel Tower at the Parisian, catch the Wynn's free "Performance Lake" fountain show. Vast, air-conditioned, surreal — and an easy warm dinner for nine at any of the food halls. Entry free · rides MOP 100–160
Macau Tower (optional)📍 Map 338m observation deck with a glass floor, and the world's highest commercial bungee if anyone's serious. Good at night. Only if you have the hour. MOP 165 (~$20)
Late ferry back to Hong Kong Sailings run until ~22:00–23:00 (some 24h from the Outer Harbour). Aim for one no later than 21:00 so nobody's wrecked for the 6am start. Passports again. HKD ~175 (~$22)

Mon · Dec 29
Bullet train to Guilin — into the karst
Rail Scenery
MorningWest Kowloon Station: mainland immigration + 240-hour transit processing, then the ~10:00 G-train.
AfternoonArrive Guilin ~13:30–14:00; Reed Flute Cave or Elephant Trunk Hill.
EveningTwo Rivers Four Lakes night cruise, then Guilin rice noodles. Overnight Guilin.
The pivot day. You leave Hong Kong on a high-speed train, clear into mainland China without a visa inside West Kowloon Station, and step off three hours later among the limestone spires that put Guilin on every Chinese banknote. One night here turns a brutal rail day into a gentle one.
⏱ Get to West Kowloon 90 minutes early. You clear Hong Kong exit immigration and Chinese entry immigration inside the station before boarding, in the co-located Mainland Port Area, and the 240-hour transit permit is issued here. With nine people and holiday-week volume, do not cut this fine. Everyone needs their passport and the confirmed onward ticket out of the mainland (see Tips → Entry). Full detail in The Rail Journey.
Hong Kong West Kowloon Station📍 Map Huge, modern, well signed. Departures ~10:01 / 11:14 / 11:52 to Guilin (verify on 12306 / Trip.com closer to the date — timetables shuffle). Eat before the gate; on-train catering is thin. The ~10:00 gets you the full Guilin afternoon. 2nd class ¥415–450 (~$58–63)
G-train: West Kowloon → Guilin West📍 Map ~3 to 3.5 hours, 580 km, up to ~200 mph. Direct — no changes. Watch the Pearl River Delta give way to karst hills as you cross into Guangxi. Guilin West is ~15 km / 25 min from the centre; grab two DiDis or taxis. Included above
Reed Flute Cave📍 Map A 240m illuminated limestone cavern — stalactites lit in violet and green, a still pool doubling the "crystal palace" chamber. Kitschy lighting, genuinely impressive rock. ~1 hour. Hours/price unverified for your dates — check locally. ¥110 (~$15)
Elephant Trunk Hill (alt to the cave)📍 Map Guilin's emblem — a rock arch on the Li River that looks exactly like an elephant drinking. You can see it well for free from the riverside park across the water or from Binjiang Road; the paid park gets you closer. 30–45 min. ¥55–90 (~$8–13)
Two Rivers & Four Lakes night cruise📍 Map ~1 hour by boat through the lit canal ring that loops central Guilin — the twin Sun and Moon Pagodas, illuminated bridges modelled on famous ones worldwide, cormorant-fishing demos. The right way to spend a single Guilin evening. Book ahead. ¥165–235 (~$23–33)
Guilin rice noodles (Guilin mǐfěn)📍 Map The local dish: rice noodles with crisp fried peanuts, pickled long beans, preserved radish and thin-sliced beef or "horse meat," in a dark spiced broth you add yourself. A few dollars a bowl at any mǐfěn shop. Late supper after the cruise. ¥12–25 (~$2–4)
Day 3 · per person~¥650–800 (~$90–110)

Guilin → Chongqing — the second leg
Rail Food
MorningSolitary Beauty Peak or a Li River viewpoint, then check out.
Midday~4-hour G-train, Guilin West → Chongqing West.
EveningCheck in, walk Jiefangbei, first Chongqing hotpot.
A half-day of Guilin, then the train to Chongqing. Pick a mid-to-late-morning departure so you get a proper Guilin morning without arriving in Chongqing at midnight — the fastest trains do it in just under 4 hours, slower ones closer to 5–6, so check the exact service.
Solitary Beauty Peak & Jingjiang Princes' City📍 Map A single limestone tower rising straight out of a Ming-dynasty walled palace compound in the city centre. 300-odd steps to a 360° view over Guilin's spires. Compact, central, doable before a train. ~1–1.5 hours. ¥100–130 (~$14–18)
Or: Li River viewpoint at Xianggong Hill (if you have a car + 3 hrs)📍 Map The classic bend-in-the-river panorama is actually downstream toward Yangshuo. A short climb at Xianggong Hill gives you the postcard shot. Only worth it if you skipped the full river cruise and have a driver — it's ~1.5 hrs each way and eats the morning. ¥30 (~$4) + car
G-train: Guilin West → Chongqing West📍 Map ~850 km. Fastest service (e.g. G320) about 3h56m; many are 4.5–6h with more stops — book a fast one. 20 pairs a day, roughly 07:15–18:05. Second class ~¥390. Chongqing West connects to metro Lines 5 and the Loop; your hotel is likely 30–45 min out. 2nd class ~¥390 (~$55)
Jiefangbei (People's Liberation Monument)📍 Map The neon heart of downtown — a 1940s clock tower now ringed by a canyon of LED-clad skyscrapers and malls. Walk it at night to get your bearings; Hongyadong is a 10-minute walk downhill from here. Free
First hotpot📍 Map Chongqing invented málà hotpot — beef-tallow broth loaded with dried chilli and numbing Sichuan peppercorn. Order a yuānyāng split pot (spicy + mild) for the group, get tripe, luncheon meat, potato, lotus root, quail eggs, and a sesame-oil-and-garlic dip. Chains like Zhou Shi Mian Kuang, Houba, or Perilla are reliable for nine. Milk tea on standby. ¥80–130 pp (~$11–18)

Wed · Dec 31
Chongqing's greatest hits — and New Year's Eve
City NYE Night
MorningCiqikou Ancient Town, then the Liziba train-through-a-building.
AfternoonYangtze River Cableway, Hongyadong by daylight, Shibati old quarter.
EveningNYE — watch Hongyadong light up from across the river, not from inside the crush.
Chongqing is a mountain city stacked in vertical layers over the confluence of the Yangtze and Jialing — escalators between "ground floors," a monorail through an apartment block, a skyline that reads as pure cyberpunk after dark. This is the sightseeing day. It's also New Year's Eve, which changes how you handle the evening.
⚠️ NYE crowds are the real hazard. Hongyadong and Jiefangbei draw enormous New Year's Eve crowds and have seen access controls and temporary closures in past years. Do not plan to be inside Hongyadong at midnight with nine people. Better: view the lit stilt-houses from the Qiansimen Bridge or the Jialing riverbank opposite, book an early-evening Yangtze night cruise, or a restaurant/bar with a view. Agree a meeting point and a "if we get separated" plan.
Ciqikou Ancient Town📍 Map A 1,000-year-old porcelain-trading port on the Jialing, now a warren of Ming-Qing lanes, teahouses and street food — máhuā fried dough twists, spicy tangyuan, hand-pulled sugar. Touristy and busy but a genuine old quarter. Go early before it packs out. ~2 hours. Free entry
Liziba Monorail Station📍 Map Line 2 runs straight through a 19-storey residential building here, with a station inside it. There's a viewing platform across the street for the shot; a train comes roughly every few minutes. Better still, ride Line 2 along the Jialing cliffside — one of the world's great urban train rides. Free to ride (¥2–5 a hop). ¥2–5 (~$0.50)
Yangtze River Cableway📍 Map A working commuter cable car strung across the Yangtze between Xinhua Road and the south bank — ~5 minutes, big views up the gorge and back at the downtown wall of towers. Queues are long midday; go earlier or buy a timed ticket. ¥30 one way. ¥30 (~$4)
Shibati (Eighteen Steps)📍 Map A restored cliffside stairway district of old Chongqing — teahouses, craftsmen, wartime-era houses, the layered-city feel without Ciqikou's crowds. Connects on foot toward the cableway's north station. ~1 hour. Free
Hongyadong (by daylight)📍 Map An 11-storey complex of traditional stilt-house architecture (diaojiaolou) built into the cliff above the Jialing — shops, food, and folk-museum floors, with entrances on multiple "ground" levels. See it now while you can move; you'll view the famous night lighting from across the water tonight. Free
NYE evening — the light show, from a safe distance📍 Map Hongyadong's gold lighting switches on around dusk. From the Qiansimen Bridge walkway or the opposite bank you get the whole cliff of lights reflected in the river, with room to breathe. A Liangjiang / Two Rivers night cruise (~1 hr, ¥120–150) is the other good call — it passes Hongyadong, Chaotianmen and the lit bridges. Free · or cruise ¥120–150 (~$17–21)

Thu · Jan 1
Day trip — Dazu Rock Carvings or Wulong Karst
Day trip UNESCO
Option ADazu Rock Carvings — 800-year-old cliff sculpture, ~1.5–2 hrs each way.
Option BWulong Karst — Three Natural Bridges & Furong Cave, ~2.5–3 hrs each way.
EveningBack in Chongqing for a slower dinner.
New Year's Day. Pick one big thing outside the city. Both are UNESCO World Heritage sites and both are a real commitment of driving/rail time — don't try to do both, and confirm holiday opening hours before you commit.
Booking note: A private van + driver for nine is the sane way to do either of these — one vehicle, door to door, waits for you. Arrange through your hotel or Trip.com a few days ahead. Public options exist (HSR to Dazu South; long-distance bus or the tourism train to Wulong) but juggling nine people and timetables on a holiday isn't worth the saving.
Option A — Dazu Rock Carvings📍 Map ~100 km west. Tens of thousands of Buddhist, Taoist and Confucian figures carved into hillsides from the 9th–13th centuries — the Baodingshan grotto is the masterpiece, a 31m Reclining Buddha and a wheel-of-life relief among them. Extraordinarily well preserved, far calmer than a city day. Allow ~3 hrs on site. Entry price/hours unverified for the holiday — check. ~¥115 (~$16) + transport
Option B — Wulong Karst📍 Map ~130 km southeast. The Three Natural Bridges — colossal limestone arches over a green sinkhole valley, a Transformers and Curse of the Golden Flower filming location — plus Furong Cave, one of China's most decorated show caves. Spectacular, more physical (lots of steps and walking), longer travel. Full day. Dress warm; the valley floor is cold in January. ~¥135 + ¥120 caves (~$18 + $17)
Back in Chongqing — quieter dinner📍 Map After a long travel day, do a sit-down Sichuan restaurant rather than hotpot again — làzi jī (chilli chicken), huíguō ròu (twice-cooked pork), kǒushuǐ jī (mouth-watering chicken), dàndàn noodles. Book for nine. ¥70–110 pp (~$10–15)

Fri · Jan 2
Museums, parks and the skyline
Culture Views
MorningThree Gorges Museum & the Great Hall of the People.
AfternoonEling Park for the ridge-top view; Chaotianmen & Raffles City observation deck.
EveningNanshan — the One Tree viewing platform for the full night skyline.
A slower, more spread-out day: the city's best museum, its grandest civic set-piece, and two of its best viewpoints — one for daylight, one for the night skyline that Chongqing is famous for.
Three Gorges Museum📍 Map Free, excellent, and the single best primer on the region — the Yangtze gorges before the dam flooded them, Ba-kingdom bronzes, and a raw permanent exhibit on Chongqing as China's wartime capital under Japanese bombing. ~2 hours. Bring passports for entry. Free
Great Hall of the People📍 Map Directly opposite the museum across a vast plaza — a 1950s domed hall modelled on Beijing's Temple of Heaven, seating 4,000. You're mainly here for the exterior and the square. 20–30 min. ¥10 to go inside (~$1.50)
Eling Park📍 Map The oldest private garden in the city, on the highest ridge of the downtown peninsula. Terraces and a pavilion looking straight down the Yangtze at the skyscrapers — a favourite local sunset spot, far less mobbed than the cableway. Free. Free
Chaotianmen & Raffles City "Crystal" skydeck📍 Map The tip of the peninsula where the two rivers meet, now crowned by Raffles City — eight towers with a horizontal glass "sky-bridge" laid across four of them at the 250m mark. Walk the observation deck for the confluence view: brown Yangtze meeting green Jialing. ¥120–150 (~$17–21)
Nanshan — One Tree Viewing Platform (Yikeshu)📍 Map On the southern mountain, the classic elevated view of the whole illuminated city wrapped around the rivers. Go up before dark, stay for the lights. Taxi/DiDi up — it's a winding climb. Pair with dinner on Nanshan's "Old Street" or the flowerpot-lined food strip. ¥30 (~$4)

Buffer day — markets, arts district, last hotpot
Food Easy
MorningArhat Temple, then Guanyinqiao for shopping and lunch.
AfternoonTestbed 2 (Erchang) arts district, or repeat a favourite.

Deliberately loose. If the earlier days ran long or someone wants a second go at Hongyadong or the cableway, this is where that slack lives. Otherwise: a temple, a shopping district, an arts complex, and a proper last hotpot.
Why a buffer day: two rail legs, a NYE crowd day and a full-day excursion is a lot of moving parts for nine people over the holidays. Something will slip. Keeping Jan 3 unscheduled means a missed train or a lost morning doesn't cascade into the departure.
Arhat Temple (Luóhàn Sì)📍 Map A working Buddhist temple wedged among downtown towers, known for its hall of 500 painted arhat statues and a rock-carving passage at the entrance. Quiet, incense-heavy, 30–45 min. Small entry fee. ¥10 (~$1.50)
Guanyinqiao pedestrian street📍 Map The other big downtown shopping zone, across the river in Jiangbei — malls, a giant open plaza, and the "Chongqing Circle" observation ring. Less touristy than Jiefangbei, good for lunch and last souvenirs. Free to wander
Testbed 2 / Erchang (former printing factory)📍 Map A 1930s munitions-then-printing works on a hillside, reborn as a design and film-set district — cafés, galleries, murals, a well-known skyline terrace. Chongqing's answer to Beijing 798, at a fraction of the size. ~1.5 hours. Free
Farewell hotpot📍 Map One more. Try a lǎo huǒguō old-style joint with the compartmented "nine-grid" pot if you haven't — different sections of the pot run at different heats. Order less than you think; it adds up fast. ¥80–130 pp (~$11–18)

Airport & station info

Hong Kong Int'l (HKG) — Airport Express is the move: 24 min to Kowloon, ~28 to Hong Kong station, HKD ~105 (cheaper for groups of 3–4 on a Group Ticket, and return-within-30-days deals exist). Taxis to TST ~HKD 270–350. Do not use unlicensed touts.
Hong Kong West Kowloon Station — the high-speed rail terminus, connected to Kowloon MTR and a short taxi from TST hotels. Mainland immigration is inside the building (Mainland Port Area). Arrive 90 min early. Food court landside; eat before you clear.
Chongqing Jiangbei Int'l (CKG) — T3A for most international flights. Metro Lines 10 & 3 connect to the city (~30–45 min); pre-booked vans ~¥120–180 for a group. Be there 3 hrs before an international departure.
Getting Around
Three payment worlds in nine days: Hong Kong (Octopus + contactless), Macao (cash / HKD / casino shuttles), mainland China (Alipay & WeChat Pay for everything). Set all of it up before you fly.

Hong Kong

🚇 MTR + Octopus
Clean, fast, covers the whole city. Get an Octopus card (or add one in Apple Wallet / Samsung Wallet) — it also pays for buses, trams, the Star Ferry, and convenience stores. Airport Express takes it too.
📱 Uber / taxi
Uber works in Hong Kong. Street taxis are cheap and metered — red = urban, carry the destination in Chinese characters. HKTaxi / Fly Taxi apps also work.
⛴ Star Ferry & trams
The ferry (HKD 5) and the double-decker "ding ding" trams (HKD 3) are transport and sightseeing. Use them.
🚌 To Macao
TurboJET (Sheung Wan → Outer Harbour) or Cotai Water Jet (→ Taipa). ~60–70 min, HKD ~175, first sailings ~07:00. Passport required.

Mainland China — Guilin & Chongqing

📱 DiDi (ride-hailing)
The Uber of China — works in-app in English, pay via Alipay/WeChat. The reliable way to move a group. Chongqing drivers navigate the multi-level roads so you don't have to.
🚇 Chongqing Rail Transit
Huge network, includes the famous cliffside monorail Lines 2 & 3 and the through-a-building Liziba stop. ¥2–7 a ride, pay with an Alipay/WeChat transit QR. Guilin's metro is minimal — you'll mostly DiDi there.
💳 Alipay + WeChat Pay
Bind a foreign Visa/Mastercard in both apps before you leave. This is how you pay for taxis, metro, noodles, tickets — cash and foreign cards are barely accepted. Carry ¥500–1,000 cash as backup.
🚄 Trains (12306)
Book HSR seats via Trip.com (English, small fee) or the official 12306 app. Passport is your ticket — scan it at the gate. Arrive 60–90 min early for security + the passport lane.

The Rail Journey
Two high-speed legs — Hong Kong → Guilin, then Guilin → Chongqing — and the border formality that happens inside a train station. This is the part that needs planning; get it right and the rest is easy.
⚠️ Book the moment the window opens. Chinese HSR tickets go on sale ~15 days ahead (sometimes up to 30 on Trip.com). You're travelling in the New Year holiday week — second class on the popular departures will sell out, and a group of nine unbooked is a real problem. Put a reminder for mid-December.
Leg 1 · West Kowloon → Guilin West
Mon Dec 29 · direct G-train · the visa-free entry point
Book first
580 km in about 3–3.5 hours, direct, no changes — the fastest service (G907/G908 family) does it in ~3 hrs. Typical departures around 10:01, 11:14 and 11:52; take the ~10:00 for a full Guilin afternoon. The 240-hour visa-free transit permit is processed here, at West Kowloon's Mainland Port Area: you clear Hong Kong exit then Chinese entry immigration inside the station before boarding. Bring every passport and the confirmed onward ticket out of the mainland. Arrive 90 minutes early — holiday-week queues plus a nine-person group. Guilin West station is ~15 km / 25 min from the city centre.

Booking & boarding — how it works

🎫
Book through Trip.com or 12306
Trip.com is English, takes foreign cards, and charges a few dollars a ticket. The official 12306 app is cheaper and now supports foreign passports but is fiddlier. Enter each passenger's name exactly as printed in the passport. For nine, book in one transaction so you're seated together.
🪪
Your passport is the ticket
No paper ticket, no collection. Scan the passport at the automated gate; foreign passengers occasionally get routed to a staffed window for a manual check, so keep 10 minutes spare. Everyone carries their own.
⏱
Arrive early
West Kowloon: 90 min (immigration + transit permit). Guilin West: 60–75 min (airport-style security, big station). Gates close 3–5 min before departure and they will leave without you.
💺
Second class is fine
2+3 seating, clean, power at every seat, tray tables. For legs under 4 hours it's not worth doubling the fare for first class — but if first-class seats are all that's left together for nine, take them.
🧳
Luggage
No check-in — you carry bags on. Racks at car ends for big cases, overhead shelves for the rest. Nine people's worth of luggage boards faster if you're grouped in one or two cars. Soft bags are easier to wedge than hard shells.
🍜
Food
Trolley service and a basic bar car exist but the food is poor and cash-light. Buy noodles, fruit, snacks and water before boarding — there's boiling water on every car for instant noodles.
Alternatives & Swap-Ins
If weather, closures, crowds or fatigue change the plan.

If the Guilin weather is grim

Skip the river, do the caves and the city
Guilin · Dec 29–30 · rain / cold plan
December in Guilin can be 5°C, grey and misty — which flatters the karst as often as it hides it, but the open-top river cruise is miserable in real rain. Fallback: Reed Flute Cave (indoors), Solitary Beauty Peak (quick), Jingjiang Princes' City, and an early night before the Chongqing train. The Two Rivers Four Lakes boats are covered and still run.

If you want more Hong Kong instead of Macao

Lantau — Big Buddha & Ngong Ping 360
Free–HKD 315 · half to full day · cable car
Trade the Macao day trip for Lantau Island: the 25-minute Ngong Ping cable car to the giant bronze Tian Tan Buddha, Po Lin Monastery's vegetarian hall, and the fishing-village stilt houses of Tai O. Less border faff, more nature, easier on a jet-lagged group. You'd lose the Portuguese-colonial contrast, which is the single most distinctive thing about the whole coast leg — so only swap if the group actively doesn't want Macao.

Chongqing day trip — the one you didn't pick

Dazu ⇄ Wulong
Jan 1 · whichever you skipped
If you chose Dazu for Jan 1 and have appetite for another excursion on the buffer day, Wulong's Three Natural Bridges are the bigger spectacle (and vice versa). Doing both means two long travel days back-to-back — fine for some groups, punishing for others. Decide based on how Jan 1 actually felt.

Chongqing rain / rest plan

Indoor Chongqing
Any Chongqing day · overcast is the default here
Chongqing is famously foggy — plan for grey. Three Gorges Museum, the Raffles City sky-bridge, Arhat Temple, a hotpot lunch that runs two hours, the Yangtze Cableway (still runs in fog, eerie), and Guanyinqiao's malls all work in bad weather. The night skyline views (Nanshan, Eling) are the only weather-dependent items.

Too tired for a 6am ferry

Compress Macao to a half-day
Dec 28 · jet-lag contingency
If Day 1 wrecks everyone, take a mid-morning ferry instead, do just the Historic Centre (Senado Square → St. Paul's → a Taipa Village lunch), skip Cotai, and be back in Hong Kong by early evening for a proper sleep before the train. Macao's old town is genuinely a 3-hour walk if you're efficient.
Tips & Practical
Entry rules first — they can stop you boarding. Everything here was checked in August 2026; the China transit rules changed twice in 2024–25, so re-verify in November.

⚠️ Entry Requirements

🛂
Hong Kong & Macao — visa-free
US passport holders: 90 days visa-free in Hong Kong, 30 days in Macao. No advance paperwork. Passport valid 6+ months beyond entry with blank pages. That's it for the coast leg.
🇨🇳
Mainland China — the 240-hour visa-free transit
This trip's plan is to enter the mainland without a visa under the 240-hour (10-day) transit scheme. As checked Aug 2026, the conditions are:

• Nationality: US is on the eligible list (~55 countries).
• Entry port: must be an approved one. Hong Kong West Kowloon Station has been approved since Nov 5, 2025 — you qualify by taking the train, clearing immigration in the station.
• Onward ticket: you must already hold a confirmed ticket, fixed date and seat, leaving the mainland within 240 hours to a third country or region — not the place you came from. Your home country counts; Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan each count as a "third region." Your Jan 4 CKG departure is the exit; have that booking with you at West Kowloon.
• Area limit: you may only move within the ~24 eligible provinces/regions. Guangxi (incl. Guilin) and Chongqing are both eligible — this itinerary stays inside the zone. A side trip to a non-eligible province would break it.
• On arrival: fill the arrival card (online/app or on paper) and register your accommodation address within 24 hours — hotels do this for you at check-in; keep the slip.
• Processing: done at the West Kowloon Mainland Port Area. Allow extra time; approval is not instant.
🧯
The zero-risk alternative — a regular tourist visa
For a group of nine, the transit scheme has no margin: one person with a vague onward ticket, a delayed exit flight, or a rule tweak between now and December, and someone gets turned away at the port. The safe route is a standard 10-year multiple-entry L (tourist) visa — apply in person at a Chinese Visa Application Service Center (there's one in Los Angeles serving Arizona; Chicago has its own), roughly $140 + ~$40 service fee, 4–7 working days, but allow weeks for nine people over the holidays. With the visa, the rail plan works exactly the same and none of the transit conditions apply. Decide by early October.
✅
Verify before you fly
Confirm current rules with your airline (they're liable if they board you wrongly and will know the port list) and China's National Immigration Administration site. Don't rely on this doc's August snapshot for a December border.

Money & Payments

💴
Three currencies
Mainland: Chinese yuan (CNY / RMB / ¥), ~¥7.15 to $1. Hong Kong: Hong Kong dollar (HKD), pegged around 7.8 to $1. Macao: pataca (MOP), ≈ HKD, and HKD is accepted everywhere in Macao at par — no need to get patacas. The converter on the Budget tab is set to CNY since that's six of the nine days.
📱
Alipay + WeChat Pay = how you pay in China
Install both and bind a foreign Visa/Mastercard before you leave — international cards have worked in both apps since 2023. On the mainland this is how you pay for taxis, the metro, street noodles, tickets, everything. Physical foreign cards are widely refused. Carry ¥500–1,000 cash per person as backup and for tiny vendors; UnionPay ATMs dispense yuan.
💳
Hong Kong & Macao are card-normal
Contactless Visa/Mastercard and Apple Pay work almost everywhere. Get an Octopus card (or virtual one in your phone wallet) for transit and convenience stores. Tell your bank you'll be in HK/Macao/China so nothing gets frozen.

Connectivity

🛜
The Great Firewall — mainland only
Google (Search, Maps, Gmail, Photos), Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook, X and most Western news are blocked in mainland China. Hong Kong and Macao are completely open. Install and test a VPN at home — you often can't download one once you're inside. A roaming eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, etc.) on a Hong Kong or regional plan frequently routes around the firewall automatically; a physical mainland SIM does not.
🗺
Maps that actually work
Google Maps is useless on the mainland. Use Amap (高德, has an English mode) or Baidu Maps; Apple Maps works acceptably. DiDi handles navigation for taxis. Download an offline map before you go.

Weather & Packing

🧥
Late December – early January
Hong Kong / Macao: mild, ~15–20°C, humid, occasional drizzle — a light jacket and a rain shell. Guilin: cold and damp, ~5–12°C, often misty — a real warm layer. Chongqing: ~6–12°C, heavily overcast (it's nicknamed "Fog City"), rarely freezing, some drizzle — warm layers, waterproof shoes for wet stone steps. No snow anywhere on this route. Bring a small umbrella.
🚻
Small stuff
Carry tissues and hand sanitizer — public toilets outside hotels are often squat-style and BYO-paper. A power bank for long sightseeing days. Universal adapter (mainland uses types A/I/C; Hong Kong uses UK-style type G — different plug).

Food — what to order

🥟
Hong Kong — dim sum & more
Har gow, siu mai, char siu bao, cheung fun, egg tarts, milk tea. Also roast goose, wonton noodles, clay-pot rice, and a bowl of congee. Yum cha (tea + dim sum) is a late-morning group meal by design.
🍮
Macao — Macanese & Portuguese
Portuguese egg tarts (Lord Stow's / Margaret's), African chicken, minchi, bacalhau cod, pork chop bun, serradura pudding. Taipa Village is the place to eat.
🍜
Guilin — rice noodles
Guilin mǐfěn: rice noodles with fried peanuts, pickled beans, preserved radish, thin beef, in a five-spice broth you ladle on yourself. A couple of dollars, eaten standing up or at a plastic stool.
🌶
Chongqing — hotpot & Sichuan
Málà hotpot is the local religion — beef-tallow broth, dried chilli, numbing peppercorn. Order a split pot (spicy + clear), a sesame-oil-garlic dip, and go easy on the order size. Also: xiǎomiàn (breakfast chilli noodles), làzi jī, huíguō ròu, kǒushuǐ jī. The spice is genuine — say "wēi là" (mild) if you mean it. Sweet soy milk or milk tea cuts the burn better than water.

New Year's Eve — Chongqing

🎆
Treat the crowds as a safety issue, not an inconvenience
Hongyadong and Jiefangbei pull massive NYE crowds and have had access limits and short-notice closures in past years. With nine people: don't be in the crush at midnight. View the lights from Qiansimen Bridge or the opposite bank, or book an early-evening river cruise or a restaurant/bar with a view. Set a meeting point and a lost-phone plan. Keep the buffer day (Jan 3) genuinely free in case NYE eats more time than planned.
