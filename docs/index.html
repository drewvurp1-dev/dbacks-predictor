Day-by-Day Itinerary
8 days / 7 nights — 5 nights Tbilisi, 2 nights Gudauri. NYE in the city, one big ski day after the rush clears. Tap any day to expand it; tap 📍 on a stop to open it in Maps.
⚠️ Standby note: Dec 31–Jan 2 has high load factors on nearly every route. Plan A is Turkish via Istanbul (PHX → ORD or DFW → IST → TBS), and those legs are daily in both directions — which matters more at your size, since nine non-revs rarely clear the same flight in holiday season. Plan on splitting across departures and regrouping in Tbilisi. Leaving PHX Dec 26 lands you in Tbilisi on Dec 28 — 01:35 or 10:05 depending on the Istanbul connection — so Day 1 below starts with a short night either way. Leave Dec 25 if you want a full recovery day first. See Getting There for the timetables.
Why the trip is shaped this way: Jan 1 is a dead day in Tbilisi — the country is home with family and most things are shut — so it's spent driving up to Gudauri, with the UNESCO sites as an opportunistic attempt en route. Two nights at the lodge bracket one full ski day on the 2nd, then the drive back down on the 3rd does the monuments properly, in daylight, with no check-in deadline. That puts you back in Tbilisi by early evening for a last supra and the souvenir run, and leaves an unhurried morning for the 11:05 flight.
👥 At 8–9 people, four things need handling. Money mostly gets better — the Gudauri Airbnb is ~$138 each and vehicles divide nicely. The four that don't look after themselves: beds for nine, a minibus rather than a car convoy for the two mountain runs, the NYE supra booked now for nine, and nine non-revs unlikely to clear the same flight. Everything else — clubbing, baths, side trips — will split naturally and just needs booking for whoever actually goes. See Travelling as a Group in Tips.

Mon · Dec 28
Arrival + First Taste of Tbilisi
Culture
MorningLand at TBS around dawn, Bolt into Old Town, drop bags and get horizontal for a few hours.
AfternoonPrivate room at the Abanotubani sulfur baths, plus a Kisa scrub to reset the travel day.
EveningEasy first dinner on Shardeni Street — khachapuri, wine, live music in the lanes.
Land, drop bags, get into the sulfur baths, and let Old Town do the rest. Don't fight the jet lag — lean into the evening. Everything today is within walking distance of the Riverview, which is the whole point of staying there.
Tbilisi International Airport (TBS) → Old Town📍 Map ~17km east of the center, roughly 25–30 min. Order a Bolt from inside the terminal — walk straight past the curbside taxi touts, who quote 3–4× the app price. The red-line metro also runs to the center for under 1 GEL if you're travelling light. Note: arrival time swings a lot by routing — a Turkish connection out of IST can land anywhere from early evening to ~01:35, while the BA fallback lands ~05:30. Message the hotel ahead about early check-in or a bag hold, and expect this day to flex around whatever you actually clear. 25–35 GEL (~$9–13)
Chreli Abano — Abanotubani Sulfur Baths📍 Map The domed brick bathhouse district below Narikala, fed by natural hot sulfur springs — the reason the city exists (Tbilisi means "warm place"). Book a private room rather than the public hall; you get your own pool, sauna, and marble slab for an hour. A room holds roughly 4–6, so book by however many of you actually want to go. Ask for the Kisa — a full-body scrub-and-soap treatment by an attendant that is not gentle and is exactly what a 20-hour travel day needs. Some rooms have rooftop pools with fortress views. 60–80 GEL/room (~$22–30) · Kisa +15–25 GEL
Shardeni Street & Erekle II📍 Map Pedestrianized lanes in the heart of Old Town, packed with wine bars, cocktail bars, and restaurants with live music spilling into the street. More tourist-facing than Sololaki or Vera, but the festive-season atmosphere is genuinely electric and it's the right low-effort first night. Order Adjaruli khachapuri — the boat-shaped one with the egg cracked in at the table. Dinner 30–60 GEL (~$11–22) · wine 8–15 GEL/glass

Tue · Dec 29
Old Town, the Fortress & First Live Music
Culture Nightlife
MorningAnchiskhati and Sioni churches while they're still empty — the oldest rooms in the city.
AfternoonCable car up to Narikala Fortress, walk the ridge to the Mother Georgia statue.
EveningMulled Saperavi at the Christmas Village, then jazz at Cafe Singer or the Fabrika courtyard.
The classic Tbilisi day — start in the quiet churches, climb above the city, then hit the Christmas market at dusk and ease into the music scene at its most relaxed. The city is fully decorated: the festive season runs Dec 12 through Jan 14.
Sioni Cathedral + Anchiskhati Basilica📍 Map Two of the city's most important churches, five minutes apart, and worth doing first thing — they're near-empty before about 10 AM and the atmosphere is completely different for it. Anchiskhati (6th century) is the oldest surviving church in Tbilisi: small, dark, and much more affecting than its size suggests. Sioni holds the grapevine cross of St. Nino, who converted Georgia in the 4th century. Shoulders and knees covered; women should carry a scarf. Free · donations welcome
Narikala Fortress + Rike Park cable car📍 Map 4th-century fortress on the ridge over Old Town. Take the cable car up from Rike Park — it's a 3-minute ride with the best view in the city, and it saves you a steep cobblestone climb. The fortress itself is free and open; walk the walls, then continue along the ridge to the aluminium Mother Georgia statue (sword in one hand for enemies, wine bowl in the other for guests — the whole national character in one sculpture). 2.5 GEL each way (~$1) · fortress free
First Republic Square — Christmas Village📍 Map Runs Dec 12–Jan 14. Market stalls, live music most nights, and mulled Saperavi — hot spiced Georgian red, sold for pocket change. This is also where the NYE Gala Concert happens on the 31st, so it's worth a scout now to see how the square is laid out. Free entry · mulled wine 2–5 GEL
🎷 Jazz Cafe Singer📍 Map Live jazz and blues in a restored historic building in Old Town. Cozy, low-key, and the gentlest possible entry into Tbilisi's music scene — no face control, no dress code, no attitude. Just Georgian wine and a good band. Music generally starts around 8 PM and it's best on weekends. Usually no cover · drinks 10–20 GEL
🏭 Fabrika📍 Map A Soviet sewing factory in Marjanishvili converted into a hostel, bar, and studio complex, built around a big graffitied courtyard that works as the social centre of young Tbilisi. The courtyard is the point — you don't need to be staying there, and it's the easiest place in the city to end up talking to locals. Either instead of the jazz or after it; it's also a short walk from the late-night khinkali places. Free entry · drinks 10–20 GEL
Day 2 · per person~60–110 GEL (~$22–41)

Wed · Dec 30
Wine, Market Food & the Sololaki Crawl
Food & Wine Nightlife
MorningOrbeliani baths, then Dry Bridge Market for Soviet antiques and the trip's souvenirs.
AfternoonDezerter Bazaar for churchkhela, spices, cheese and general market chaos. Cash only.
EveningSololaki cellar wine crawl → live music at Mtkvarze → late-night khinkali run.
The best day of the trip if you like eating and drinking. Slow morning in the baths, chaotic food market at midday, then a cellar-to-cellar wine crawl that rolls into live music on the river and ends with the late-night khinkali run.
Orbeliani Baths📍 Map The one with the blue-and-turquoise tiled façade — the most photographed building in Abanotubani, done in Persian style. A second bath session sounds excessive until you've had one; it's the standard local hangover-and-life-reset move. Morning slot, before the markets. 60–80 GEL/room (~$22–30)
🕰 Dry Bridge Market📍 Map An open-air flea market where people sell off the Soviet Union — medals, film cameras, samovars, porcelain, propaganda posters, sheet music, chandeliers. Half junk, half remarkable, and the best souvenir stop in the country by a distance. Haggling expected and friendly. Do your souvenir buying here: it's a daytime market, so it's shut by the time you're back from the mountains on the 3rd. Cash, and give it an hour or two.

Two markets in one morning is a lot of walking — if you're flagging, this is the one to trim, and Old Town shops can cover souvenirs on the last night. Free to browse · buy what you like
Dezerter Bazaar📍 Map Tbilisi's sprawling, half-covered central market next to the train station — spice pyramids, wheels of sulguni cheese, dried fruit, walnuts, cured meat, and strings of churchkhela hanging like candles (walnuts threaded on string, dipped repeatedly in thickened grape juice — Georgia's original energy bar). Buy churchkhela here rather than the tourist shops in Old Town; it's a third of the price and fresher. Cash only. Churchkhela 3–8 GEL · browse budget 20–40 GEL
🍷 Vino Underground + Sololaki cellar crawl📍 Map Sololaki is the 19th-century Art Nouveau quarter below Narikala — crumbling grand facades, carved wooden doorways, and brick cellars converted into wine bars. Vino Underground is the anchor: a candlelit basement with one of the country's best natural-wine lists, all qvevri (fermented underground in clay vessels — an 8,000-year-old method). Order an amber wine — white grapes fermented on the skins, which is Georgia's actual signature style, not the reds. Then wander; the side streets off Gorgasali Square hide half a dozen more. 8–15 GEL/glass · degustation flight 30–60 GEL
🎸 Mtkvarze — live music on the riverbank📍 Map A converted Soviet-era restaurant building on the bank of the Mtkvari, now the city's best mixed-genre live venue — Georgian folk, jazz, occasional rock. Local crowd, no tourist markup, and the setting is the selling point: Metekhi Church floodlit on the cliff above, river below, Old Town on both sides. Doors around 8 PM. Cover 0–20 GEL · drinks 10–20 GEL
🥟 Late-night khinkali run📍 Map The canonical end to a Tbilisi night. Fat pleated dumplings full of spiced meat broth — hold by the topknot, bite a small hole, drink the broth first, then eat the rest and leave the doughy knot on your plate. The knots are how you count what you've eaten. Never use a fork; Georgians will notice. The specialists around Marjanishvili and near Fabrika stay open to 2–3 AM specifically for this. Order 5–7 per person. ~5 GEL each · 25–40 GEL/person
Day 3 · per person~150–250 GEL (~$56–93)

Thu · Dec 31
New Year's Eve in Tbilisi 🎆
Major Event
MorningSleep in. Shops shut early today — grab anything you need before mid-afternoon.
AfternoonChronicle of Georgia — sixteen 30m monoliths on a hill, free, with no opening hours to be closed.
EveningThe supra — a full Georgian feast with a Tamada running toasts. Book weeks ahead.
MidnightCitywide fireworks from the ridge, then a club if the door lets you in — khinkali either way.
New Year's Eve is the holiday in Georgia — considerably bigger than Christmas. Families run enormous supras, the whole city sets off fireworks simultaneously at midnight (not a single organized display — thousands of them, from every balcony and courtyard), and the celebration rolls on for days.
🗿 Chronicle of Georgia📍 Map Sixteen black steel-and-stone monoliths, 30m tall, carved with Georgian kings down one face and scenes from the life of Christ down the other, on a hill above the Tbilisi Sea reservoir. Unfinished, unfenced, almost never crowded, and genuinely staggering in scale. It slots in here precisely because today is awkward — shops shut early and the supra isn't until evening, and the Chronicle has no ticket office and no opening hours to be closed. Go for the late-afternoon light; the stone takes it beautifully. About 30 minutes out by Bolt — have the driver wait, or you'll be stranded on an empty hilltop. Free · Bolt ~15–20 GEL each way — have it wait
⚠️ Traditional Supra Feast — BOOK 3–4 WEEKS AHEAD A supra is the formal Georgian feast: a table loaded end-to-end with khachapuri, khinkali, grilled meats, walnut-sauced vegetables, and endless wine, run by a Tamada (toastmaster) who delivers structured toasts all night and expects everyone to drink to each one. A group of 8–9 is actually the right size for this — a supra for two is a slightly sad thing, and this is the one context where your headcount is a pure advantage.

⚠️ But book it now, and book it for nine. NYE set menus sell out weeks ahead at two covers; at nine you are competing for one of a handful of tables that can seat you at all, and you may need a private room. This is the single most important reservation of the trip — more so than the hotel, and by some distance the one most likely to fall through if left. 100–250 GEL/person (~$37–93) · NYE set menu
First Republic Square — Gala Concert + midnight fireworks📍 Map The main public celebration: big Georgian pop acts, countdown, and the citywide fireworks at midnight. Get somewhere with a view — the Narikala ridge and the bridges over the Mtkvari are spectacular, because you can see the entire city going off at once. Metro, buses, and the Rike ropeway run free until 3 AM. Free · transit free until 3 AM
🎧 After midnight: a club, maybe (genuinely optional)📍 Map Don't build the night around getting into Bassiani. Its door is famously selective, and while there's no formal nationality rule, reviews from foreign visitors turned away without explanation are common enough to plan around — see the Nightlife section in Tips for the detail. Register at bassiani.com days ahead, dress dark and plain, don't roll up as an obvious all-male tourist group, and treat getting in as a bonus rather than the plan.

Go in twos and threes, not as a block — Tbilisi door staff turn away large groups on principle, so whoever wants to go should arrive separately rather than queueing as one lump. That's the only group-size adjustment this needs.

Realistic order: Café Gallery in Old Town first — same electronic programming, the most accessible door, and central enough that a refusal doesn't strand you. Khidi second: easier than Bassiani but no guarantee, and one report has foreigners quoted 150 GEL. Bassiani only as a gamble you're happy to lose.

The fireworks and the 3 AM khinkali are the night, and those work brilliantly for the whole group. The club is for whoever still wants it at 2 AM. Entry 20–40 GEL (~$7–15) · possibly more as a foreigner
🥟 Post-club khinkali, 3–5 AM📍 Map Mandatory. Marjanishvili, near Fabrika. You'll know you've found the right place when half the tables are people still in club clothes. 25–40 GEL (~$9–15)
Day 4 · per person~150–330 GEL (~$56–122)

Fri · Jan 1
New Year's Day — Up the Military Highway
Travel Day
MorningSlow start, checkout. The city is shut and the roads are as empty as they get.
AfternoonMtskheta, Jvari and Ananuri en route — if they're open. If not, they keep till the 3rd.
EveningInto Gudauri, check into the lodge, sort tomorrow's gear rental tonight.
New Year's Day in Georgia is a national write-off — shops shut, restaurants closed, everyone home with family — which makes it a good day to be in a car. The Military Highway is about as quiet as it ever gets. The three sights en route are the low-stakes attempt: try them today, and anything that's closed carries over to the drive back down on the 3rd.
🕍 Will they actually be open? Probably — these are working religious sites, not businesses, and the Georgian Orthodox Church runs on the Julian calendar, where New Year falls on Jan 14, not Jan 1. So today isn't a religious holiday for them and active churches generally keep ordinary daily hours. Ananuri's grounds are open and free regardless. That said, hours around a public holiday can be irregular and none of this is verifiable in advance — so treat anything open today as a bonus, not the plan. The guaranteed slot is the 3rd, and it's the better one anyway: no luggage, no check-in deadline, nowhere to be.
🚗 GoTrip — Tbilisi → Gudauri, with stops One booking for the whole run, stops and waiting included in the fixed price. Specify Mtskheta, Jvari, and Ananuri when you book so the route is priced right, even if you end up skipping them. Request a minibus, not sedans — a standard GoTrip car seats 3–4, so nine people means a three-vehicle convoy with three drivers making independent calls about stops. Book this one well in advance: Jan 1 is a public holiday, drivers are thinner on the ground, and larger vehicles are the first thing to go. ~$60/car (~160 GEL, split)
Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, Mtskheta (if open)📍 Map Twenty minutes out of Tbilisi in the ancient capital. UNESCO-listed, 11th century, and the holiest church in Georgia — tradition holds Christ's robe is buried beneath it. A guide changes this stop more than any other on the trip, but a guide is unlikely to be available today; another argument for saving it for the 3rd. Free entry
Jvari Monastery (if open)📍 Map 590–605 AD, UNESCO-listed, on a bluff above the confluence of the Aragvi and Mtkvari — the two rivers run side by side in different colours before merging. Ten minutes above Mtskheta. Even if the church itself is locked, the viewpoint is outdoors and worth the detour. Free
Ananuri Fortress📍 Map A 17th-century fortified church complex on the Zhinvali reservoir, directly on the highway. The grounds are open and free with no ticket office, so this is the one most likely to work today whatever else is shut. Two churches inside the curtain wall, one with vivid surviving frescoes. Free
Arrive Gudauri — check into the lodge📍 Map Two nights here, so unpack properly. Sort the gear rental tonight if any shop is still open — tomorrow is the only ski day and you want the first lift, not a queue at 10 AM. Hit an ATM in Tbilisi before you leave; smaller Gudauri restaurants and rental shops are cash-only. Eat at the lodge tonight — Jan 1 in a ski village is not a night for hunting restaurants. Dinner 40–70 GEL (~$15–26)
Day 5 · per person~40–90 GEL + car share (~$15–33)

Sat · Jan 2
Bedoba — The Ski Day
Skiing Culture
MorningFirst lift at 10:00. Saturday in holiday season — be there when they open.
AfternoonKobi–Gudauri gondola for the Kazbek panorama; optional tandem paraglide off the ridge.
EveningEveryone back together for après and a second night up here. Nothing to drive, nowhere to be.
January 2nd is Bedoba — the "day of luck," when Georgians hold that however you spend the day sets the tone for your whole year. Skiing counts; so does a slow morning and a good view, which is convenient given 2–3 of you aren't skiing. Lifts run 10:00–17:00. The best structural feature of this version of the trip is what happens afterwards: nothing. Everyone converges on the bar and stays there.
⚠️ Busiest day of the week. A Saturday inside the New Year holiday window is the fullest Gudauri gets — expect queues at the main lifts by mid-morning. Be at the base for the 10:00 opening, and have the gear sorted the night before so you're not standing in a rental line while the good snow gets tracked out.
Gudauri Ski Resort📍 Map 35km of groomed piste topping out at 3,276m, plus the off-piste and freeride terrain that gets the Caucasus mentioned alongside Japan and BC. Snow depth typically 1–2m. Almost entirely above the treeline, so wide-open bowls rather than cut runs, on modern lifts. Northern slopes hold the best snow. Take the Kobi–Gudauri gondola at least once for the Kazbek panorama — on a single ski day it's the thing not to skip. Day pass 50–70 GEL (~$18–26)
Gear rental Skis or board, boots, poles, helmet, from the shops at the base. Bring your own helmet if you have one. Goggles and SPF 30+ are non-negotiable at this altitude even under cloud. Sort it the night before. 40–60 GEL/day (~$15–22)
On-mountain food, drinks & après Mountain restaurants during the day, base-area bars from 17:00. Après can run properly tonight — you're sleeping here and driving nowhere, which was not true in the earlier version of this plan. 50–80 GEL (~$18–30)
🧘 For the 2–3 not skiing Worth being straight about this: Gudauri is a purpose-built ski resort, not a charming alpine village. There's no old town to wander and not much of a high street. If you're not on the mountain, the day is what you make of it — which suits a relaxation-focused day fine, but it needs a plan rather than an assumption. The genuinely good options:

1. The Russia–Georgia Friendship Monument — a huge curved concrete amphitheatre lined with a vivid Soviet-era mosaic, perched over the Devil's Valley on the Military Highway a few minutes north of the resort. One of the most striking things in the country, and completely accessible without skis. 📍 Map — arrange a short taxi, or ask the Airbnb host.

2. Ride the Kobi–Gudauri gondola as a foot passenger. No lift pass needed for a sightseeing ticket, and the Kazbek panorama from the top is the best view up here by some way.

3. Tandem paragliding — see below. It needs no skiing ability at all, and honestly suits the non-skiers better than someone giving up slope time for it.

4. Spa, sauna, and a window. Check what the Airbnb has; several Gudauri hotels sell spa access to non-guests. On a clear day the terrace views alone justify a slow afternoon. Gondola sightseeing ~15–30 GEL · monument free
🪂 Optional: tandem paragliding📍 Map Launch off the ridge and fly the Caucasus with the valley underneath, 10–20 minutes airborne. Weather-dependent and cancels readily on winter wind. On a single ski day, booking and waiting can eat two hours of slope time — worth deciding in advance whether you'd rather have the flight or the runs. ~299 GEL (~$111) · optional
Day 6 · per person~140–210 GEL (~$52–78) · +299 if flying

Sun · Jan 3
Down the Highway — Monuments, then Last Night in Tbilisi
UNESCO Sites
MorningUnhurried breakfast and checkout. No lifts to catch, no alarm.
AfternoonAnanuri on the reservoir, then Svetitskhoveli and Jvari at Mtskheta — in daylight.
EveningInto Tbilisi around 18:00. Farewell supra and a last run at the Old Town shops.
This is the good version of the drive, and the reason the monuments belong on the way down rather than the way up: no luggage-and-check-in rush at the far end, no deadline, and a Sunday, so Svetitskhoveli will have services running. Everything in daylight, then back into the city with an evening to spend.
⏱ Leave Gudauri around 12:00–13:00. Sunset is about 17:30. A midday departure puts you at Ananuri ~13:00–14:00, Mtskheta and Jvari ~15:00–16:30, and into Tbilisi 17:30–18:30 — which is the 6-ish you were aiming for, with everything still lit. Pushing for a 19:00 arrival means doing Mtskheta after dark, and Jvari is a viewpoint — in the dark there is nothing to see.
🚗 GoTrip — Gudauri → Tbilisi, with stops Second booking, same model: stops and waiting included. Minibus again — this run has three sightseeing stops, and keeping nine people on one vehicle is the difference between a coordinated day and herding a convoy. Specify Ananuri and Mtskheta so the route prices correctly. Confirm the driver the night before, and check GeoRoad before you set off — this descent is in daylight, which is a real improvement, but it's still a winter mountain road. ~$60/car (~160 GEL, split)
Ananuri Fortress📍 Map First stop coming down, about 45 minutes below Gudauri. A 17th-century fortified church complex on the Zhinvali reservoir — two churches inside the curtain wall, one with vivid surviving frescoes and a carved grapevine cross on the façade. The reservoir in low winter light is worth the stop by itself. Allow 45 minutes. Free
Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, Mtskheta📍 Map UNESCO-listed, 11th century, the holiest church in Georgia — tradition holds that Christ's robe is buried beneath it. Vast interior, surviving frescoes. Hire a guide at the site: without one it's handsome stonework, with one it's the founding story of Georgian Christianity. It's a Sunday, so there may be a service on — worth timing around, or worth walking into, depending on how you feel about it. Shoulders and knees covered; a scarf for women. Free entry · guide ~90–120 GEL (~$33–44) for the group
Jvari Monastery📍 Map 590–605 AD, UNESCO-listed, on a bluff directly above where the Aragvi and Mtkvari meet — you can see the two rivers running side by side in different colours before they merge. Ten minutes above Mtskheta and the best viewpoint of the trip. The wind up there in winter is serious. Do this one before the light goes; it's the whole reason for the timing note above. Free
⚠️ Where is everyone sleeping tonight? The Tbilisi Airbnb runs Dec 27 – Jan 1 and the Gudauri one ended this morning, so tonight is currently unbooked. Front-runner is the HGI Riverview for the single night, on the friends-and-family rate; extending the Airbnb through Jan 4 is the tidier alternative if the calendar allows. Either way settle it in advance — you're arriving around 18:00 with a supra and a souvenir run to fit in, and you don't want to be hunting for keys at 19:00 with nine people and ski bags. ~$150–250 for the group
🍽 Farewell supra in Tbilisi Last proper Georgian meal. Order what you haven't got to: ostri (spicy beef stew), chashushuli, badrijani nigvzit (aubergine rolls with walnut paste), and a bottle of Saperavi. Old Town or Sololaki, both a short walk from the hotel. 40–80 GEL (~$15–30)
🎁 Last souvenirs — Old Town shops Note that Dry Bridge Market is long shut by now — it's a daytime flea market, which is exactly why the serious souvenir shopping sits on Day 3. What's still open this evening are the Old Town shops around Shardeni and Erekle II and along Rustaveli: bottled wine and chacha, churchkhela, cloisonné enamel, felt, carpets. Wine shops will vacuum-pack a bottle for the hold if you ask. Whatever you're willing to carry
Day 7 · per person~100–180 GEL + car share (~$37–67)

Unhurried Departure

The payoff for routing through Istanbul. TK379 departs 11:05, so you're out of the hotel around 08:20 and at the airport by 09:00 — an ordinary morning rather than a pre-dawn scramble. Enough time for a proper breakfast and one more walk through Old Town before the ride out.

📌 Missing by three days: Orthodox Christmas, Jan 7 Georgia follows the Julian calendar, so Christmas is Jan 7 — and the Alilo procession (thousands in costume chanting through Old Tbilisi from First Republic Square to Sameba Cathedral, from around 14:00) is one of the country's great public spectacles. A Jan 4 departure misses it. If the flights ever allow a three-day extension, it's the single best reason to take it. Free · requires extending to Jan 7+

Getting There
PHX → TBS on Turkish via Istanbul. Timetables below; all times are local to the airport, +1 marks a next-day arrival.
⚠️ Departure-date math: the connection out of Istanbul is what sets your Tbilisi arrival, and it's later than it looks. Leaving PHX on Dec 26 puts you into Tbilisi on Dec 28 — either 01:35 in the small hours or 10:05 mid-morning, depending which IST connection you take. To land on Dec 27 and get a full recovery day before Day 1, you'd need to leave PHX on Dec 25.

👥 At 8–9 non-revs, treat all of this as per-sub-group rather than per-trip. Nine seats clearing on one holiday flight is unlikely; expect to travel in twos and threes across different departures and possibly different days, and to converge in Tbilisi. Build the ground bookings so a late arrival costs one person a day, not the group its reservations.

Both are daily, and both feed the same 22:15 IST→TBS departure, so either gateway lands you in Tbilisi at 01:35. The difference is margin: ORD's long layover is recovery room if you don't clear a flight, while DFW's three hours leaves little. On revenue tickets DFW would be the better connection; on standby, ORD's slack is worth more than the shorter day.

Alliances don't govern non-rev — ZED agreements do. Turkish being Star Alliance rather than oneworld doesn't by itself put it out of reach: employee standby runs on bilateral ZED agreements between individual carriers, which cross alliance lines freely. Connecting at Istanbul

IST is enormous — one of the largest terminals in the world, and transfers between Turkish flights are airside, so there's no immigration queue on a same-day connection. Allow more walking time than the signage suggests. On the 7h25m ORD layover you can clear landside into the city, but budget 3 hrs round trip and remember you'd be re-clearing security. Turkish operates a transit hotel inside the terminal, and longer layovers may qualify for their complimentary TourIstanbul tours — ask at the transfer desk rather than assuming.
Verify before booking. These are current published timetables and airlines reshuffle seasonally — re-check every time above closer to departure, especially the IST→TBS departures, which are the load-bearing part of this plan.

Swap Into a Tbilisi Day

Mtatsminda Park + funicular📍 Map
Funicular ~5–10 GEL · 2–3 hrs
The funicular climbs from the city to a ridgetop amusement park at 770m with a Ferris wheel and the widest view over Tbilisi. Faintly surreal in winter, mostly empty, and the ride itself is the attraction. The Pantheon of Writers — where much of Georgian literary and political history is buried — is at the mid-station.
Georgian National Museum📍 Map
~15–25 GEL · Rustaveli Ave · 1.5–2 hrs
Go for two things: the Treasury of pre-Christian Colchian goldwork (2nd millennium BC, and extraordinary), and the Museum of Soviet Occupation upstairs, which is a bracing corrective to any romantic notions about the period. A good rainy-day or hangover option since it's indoors and central.

Day Trips Out of the City

Kakheti wine region — Sighnaghi, Bodbe, Telavi📍 Map
Group tour ~$13–39pp · private from ~$99 · full day, ~2 hrs each way
The obvious big swap. Kakheti produces most of Georgia's wine, and Sighnaghi is a walled hilltop town overlooking the Alazani Valley with the Caucasus behind it. Winter means bare vines but also empty tasting rooms and full attention from the winemakers. Tasting costs vary sharply by venue: family maranis are often free or 10–15 GEL with snacks, large factory tastings 15–25 GEL, boutique producers 30–60 GEL. Add Bodbe Monastery (St. Nino's burial place) which is usually bundled in.
⚠️ Kazbegi / Stepantsminda + Gergeti Trinity📍 Map
Group ~$13–39pp · 4x4 up to church ~20 GEL · full day, ~3 hrs each way
The postcard shot of Georgia: a 14th-century stone church alone on a ridge at 2,170m with the 5,054m glaciated cone of Mt. Kazbek behind it. The winter caveat is real — the road closures on the Military Highway hit exactly the stretch north of Gudauri toward Kazbegi, so this trip can be cancelled at short notice by snow. You now have two Gudauri nights (Jan 1 and Jan 2) but only one ski day, so this is a genuine option: it would mean trading the ski day on the 2nd, not adding a night. Kazbegi is about an hour further north of Gudauri. Only commit on a clear forecast — this is exactly the stretch of road that closes — and check GeoRoad first. The final climb to the church needs a 4x4 in winter; don't plan to walk it.
Uplistsikhe cave city + Gori📍 Map
Entries ~15–25 GEL each · full day, ~1.5 hrs each way
A rock-hewn town carved into a cliff face, inhabited from roughly the 1st millennium BC — halls, wine cellars, a theatre, and a pagan temple all cut from living stone. Pairs with Gori, Stalin's birthplace, where the museum is a genuinely strange experience: still largely presented as it was in Soviet times, with his preserved birth house and personal railway carriage. Uncomfortable and worth seeing.
Borjomi📍 Map
Park entry ~2–7 GEL · full day, ~2 hrs each way
The mineral-water spa town, in a forested gorge in the Lesser Caucasus. Faded imperial-resort architecture, a central park where you drink the famously sulfurous water straight from the spring, and open-air warm pools. A gentler day than the mountains — good if someone in the group wants a rest day that isn't just sitting in Tbilisi.

Winter & Active

Tandem paragliding over Gudauri📍 Map
~299 GEL/person · 10–20 min flight
Launch off the ridge and fly the Caucasus with the valley underneath. One of the most accessible tandem operations anywhere and cheap by alpine standards. Weather-dependent and cancels readily on winter wind, so book the day before and hold it loosely. Already flagged as optional on Day 6.
Heli-skiing from Gudauri
Price on enquiry · full day
Gudauri runs one of the world's more affordable heli operations — a fraction of Alaska or BC pricing for comparable Caucasus terrain. Not a casual add: it needs a booking, avalanche kit, and confident off-piste skiing. Enquire at the resort the evening before, or through a local operator in advance if it's a priority.
Snowmobiling & snowshoeing, Gudauri
Varies · 1–3 hrs
The fallback if the lifts shut on wind — which happens at Gudauri, since it's mostly above the treeline and exposed. Both are bookable at the base area on the day. Useful to know about before you find yourself with a paid lift pass and closed lifts.

If the Trip Ever Extends

Jan 7 — Orthodox Christmas & the Alilo procession📍 Map
Free · ~2 PM, First Republic Square → Sameba Cathedral
Georgia keeps the Julian calendar, so Christmas falls on Jan 7. Thousands process through Old Tbilisi in costume, chanting, carrying banners, collecting alms for the poor, ending at the enormous Sameba Cathedral. It is the single most striking public event of the Georgian year and a Jan 4 departure misses it by three days. If the standby flights ever make a longer trip viable, this is the reason.
Mestia & Svaneti📍 Map
8–10 hrs from Tbilisi · needs 3+ days
Medieval stone defensive towers scattered through high Caucasus villages, plus Hatsvali and Tetnuldi ski areas and world-class backcountry. Too far to bolt onto this trip — it's the reason for a second one. Flights from Tbilisi to Mestia exist and cut the journey dramatically, but they're small aircraft and weather-cancel often.
Batumi📍 Map
5–6 hrs by train/road · needs 2+ days
Black Sea coast — subtropical, palm trees, a boulevard of aggressively strange modern architecture, and a casino scene. Very quiet out of season, which some people prefer. The overnight or fast train from Tbilisi is comfortable and cheap. Off-plan for a winter trip built around snow, but worth knowing it exists.

Holidays & Calendar

🎄

Celebrated but subdued. Christmas Village at First Republic Square is already in full swing (runs Dec 12–Jan 14). Mulled wine, markets, live music nightly.

NYE is bigger than Christmas in Georgia. Elaborate family supras (feasts), citywide fireworks at midnight, free public transport until 3 AM. Book restaurants 3–4 weeks ahead minimum. Christmas Village hosts the main Gala Concert.
🍀
Jan 2 — Bedoba (Day of Luck)
How you spend this day is said to define your whole year. Georgians keep it calm — brunch, spa, relaxed gatherings. Perfect excuse to do a morning bath session before heading to the mountains.
⛪

Georgia uses the Julian calendar — Christmas falls on Jan 7. The Alilo Procession starts at ~2pm from First Republic Square, ending at Sameba Cathedral. Thousands chanting through Old Tbilisi's cobblestone streets. One of Georgia's most remarkable public events. Consider extending the trip to catch it.

Travelling as a Group of 8–9

Only the things everyone does together are listed here. Plenty of the trip will naturally split — clubbing, baths, paragliding, whoever fancies a museum — and those need no special handling beyond booking for the number who actually turn up. What follows is the short list of things where the full headcount genuinely constrains the plan.
💰
The money mostly gets better, not worse
Everything shared divides by nine: the Gudauri Airbnb works out at ~$138 each for two nights on the New Year weekend, and the vehicles land around $30–45 a head. What scales badly is anything per person — the NYE supra, lift passes, and eight or nine dinners a night. Overall you're at roughly $498–719 each on the ground.
🚐
Book vehicles by capacity, not by trip
A Bolt holds 4. A GoTrip sedan holds 3–4. Every city movement is 2–3 cars, and the two mountain runs need a minibus unless you want a three-car convoy with three drivers improvising the stops between them. Request the larger vehicle explicitly when booking GoTrip — and book the Jan 1 leg early, since it's a public holiday and big vehicles go first.
🍽
Reserve everything. Nine people don't walk in.
In holiday season, a party of nine turning up unannounced at a good Tbilisi restaurant will not be seated. The NYE supra is the critical one — book it now, for nine, and expect to need a set menu or a private room. Apply the same rule to the farewell dinner on Jan 3 and anywhere you actually care about eating. The Sololaki wine bars are cellars: small, and not built for nine at once — expect to split across two tables or two bars.
🏨
⚠️ Mind the gap on the night of Jan 3
The two Airbnbs cover Dec 27 – Jan 1 in Tbilisi and Jan 1 – Jan 3 in Gudauri. That leaves the night of Jan 3 unbooked, when you come back down for the last evening. Two routes:

① Extend the Airbnb through Jan 4 — cleanest if the calendar allows, since you keep the same keys and don't move bags twice.
② Hilton Garden Inn Riverview for the one night — the current front-runner. It's in the right part of town (walkable to Old Town, near the baths), and it's where the friends-and-family rate applies. At 8–9 people that's 4–5 rooms for a single night; worth confirming the discount covers a block that size, since these rates often cap at one or two rooms.

Either way, don't leave it — nine beds at short notice in the first week of January is not a day-of problem to solve.
✈️
⚠️ Nine non-revs on one flight is the real risk
This is the part of the plan your group size genuinely threatens. Standby clears by seat availability, and nine open seats on the same holiday-period flight is a very different proposition from two. Expect the group to split across departures, and possibly across days. Worth deciding in advance: does everyone wait for the whole group, or do people travel in sub-groups and meet in Tbilisi? Turkish's daily frequency helps a lot — but plan for the split rather than being surprised by it, and make sure the Gudauri and NYE bookings don't collapse if two people arrive a day late.

Money & Payments

💳
Currency: Georgian Lari (GEL)
1 USD ≈ 2.7 GEL. Cards widely accepted in Tbilisi hotels, restaurants, and Gudauri lift ticket offices. Smaller mountain restaurants and marshrutka drivers are cash only. Withdraw GEL from Tbilisi ATMs before heading to the mountains. Bring backup cash from PHX.
🚕
Never take a curbside taxi
Unmetered, tourist pricing is common. Always use Bolt in cities. Airport curbside taxi touts are especially aggressive — walk past them to Bolt pickup zones or metro.

Food & Drink Must-Tries

🧀
Khachapuri
Georgia's iconic cheese bread. The Adjarian variety (Adjaruli) is a boat-shaped bread loaded with melted cheese, butter, and a raw egg cracked in at the table. Non-negotiable order.
🥟
Khinkali
Georgian soup dumplings. Hold by the knob, bite a small hole, suck the broth, then eat the rest. Do not eat the knob — it's a handle, not food. Pasanauri town on the drive to Gudauri is famous for its khinkali; stop for lunch.
🍷
Qvevri Wine
Georgia has 8,000 years of winemaking history. Qvevri wines are fermented underground in large clay vessels — especially the amber/orange wines made from white grapes with skin contact. Rkatsiteli and Mtsvane are the classic Georgian white varieties; Saperavi is the signature red. Always order a degustation in a Tbilisi wine cellar.
🌰
Churchkhela
Georgia's "Snickers" — walnuts or hazelnuts threaded on a string and dipped in thickened grape juice. Found at every market. Buy from Dezerter Bazaar, not tourist shops.

Packing for Winter

🧥
Tbilisi: Chilly European winter
December averages 7°C (45°F) highs, dropping to -2°C (28°F) overnight. January is colder: ~4°C (39°F) highs. Pack a real winter coat, warm layers, and waterproof shoes for cobblestone streets. About 51% clear/sunny days in December — don't expect constant gray.
⛷️
Gudauri: Full mountain kit
Ski gear rentable on-site (40–60 GEL/day). Bring your own helmet if you have one — rental helmets at mountain resorts worldwide are hit or miss. Sunglasses and SPF 30+ essential at altitude even in winter. Layers: resort base area is warmer than on-piste.
👗
Modest dress for religious sites
Shoulders and knees covered for Mtskheta, Ananuri, and any churches. Women should have a scarf for head covering inside active churches. This applies to Jvari Monastery, Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, and Gergeti Trinity if you add Kazbegi.

Nightlife

🍷
Wine Bars — Sololaki & Vera (Start Here)
The best casual nightlife in Tbilisi. Underground brick cellars serving natural qvevri wines by the glass (8–15 GEL). Vino Underground in Sololaki is the anchor — one of Georgia's best natural wine lists in a candlelit cellar. Side streets off Gorgasali Square have additional gems. The Vera district is more local and slightly less polished — both worth exploring. Bars open ~7 PM and run late.
🎷
Jazz Cafe Singer — Live Jazz in Old Town
Live jazz and blues in a beautifully restored historic building. Cozy, soulful, no agenda. The most relaxed music venue in the city — no face control, no dress code, just Georgian wine and good music. Best on weekends. Open from ~8 PM.
🎸
Mtkvarze — Live Folk, Jazz & Rock on the River
Tbilisi's best live music venue for mixed genres — Georgian folk acts, jazz, and rock on the banks of the Mtkvari. Informal and local without tourist-bar pricing. The setting is remarkable: Metekhi Church lit up on the cliff above, Old Town on both sides. Open ~8 PM.
🎨
✅ Café Gallery — Start Here
Art space meets electronic music in Old Town, and the one to actually plan around. Same broad electronic programming as the bigger rooms, a door policy that doesn't treat foreigners as a problem to be screened, and a central location — so if it doesn't work out you're a short walk from the wine bars rather than stranded outside a stadium at 3 AM. Less mythology than Bassiani, far less risk of standing in the cold for an hour to be refused. The sensible NYE plan: here first, Khidi if you want more, Bassiani only as a gamble you're happy to lose.
🎧
⚠️ Bassiani — The Techno Club (Hard Door, No Guarantees)
Ranked alongside Berghain as one of the world's great techno clubs. Housed in the former Olympic swimming pool under Dinamo Arena — a huge concrete space with an immersive system. Fri & Sat only, midnight to 10–11 AM.

⚠️ The door is the problem, and it's worth being realistic about. There's no official rule barring foreigners, and foreign passports aren't automatically refused — but door staff are explicitly screening for whether you're there for the music or treating the club as a tourist attraction, and foreign visitors get noticeably more scrutiny. Reviews describing tourists turned away with no reason given are common, and some go further, alleging outright preference for Georgian citizens. Russian passport holders are refused as a matter of course.

Some context, without excusing the profiling reports: Bassiani is a political space as much as a club. It has been a refuge for Georgia's queer community and was raided by armed police in 2018, triggering the protests known as the "rave-olution." A hard door is how that space protects itself. That's a real reason it's difficult — and it also means a rejection isn't personal.

If you try: register at bassiani.com days ahead (ID and a social account are required), dress dark and plain, arrive after 3 AM, act sober in the queue, and don't turn up as a large all-male group. Camera taped at the door. Go with a fallback already agreed and treat entry as a bonus.
🌉
Khidi — Under the Bridge
Occupies the underside of the Vakhushti Bagrationi Bridge over the Mtkvari, with outdoor decks and a harder industrial aesthetic. Similar programming to Bassiani and a genuinely easier door — but easier is not the same as easy. Face control still reads your outfit, reviews advise against turning up as an all-male group, and at least one visitor reports being quoted 150 GEL at the door once staff realised they weren't Georgian — well above the usual 20–40. Better odds than Bassiani, not a guarantee, and budget for the possibility of foreigner pricing.
🥟
🌟 MUST: Late-Night Khinkali Run
The canonical post-bar, post-club food in Tbilisi. Late-night khinkali specialists near Fabrika and in the Marjanishvili area are open until 2–3 AM specifically to feed people coming off a long night. How to eat them: Hold by the topknot, bite a small hole, drink the broth first, then eat the dumpling. Leave the doughy knot on the plate — it's a counter of how many you've had. Do not use a fork. Georgians will notice.
💰
Nightlife Budget
A full evening — dinner, wine bar, and club — runs 80–150 GEL/person ($29–55 USD). Wine by the glass: 8–15 GEL. Craft beer: 8–12 GEL. Cocktails: 15–25 GEL. Club entry: 20–40 GEL normally, though foreigners have reported being quoted up to 150 GEL at Khidi. Late-night khinkali: ~5 GEL each. One of the best-value nightlife cities in Europe.
