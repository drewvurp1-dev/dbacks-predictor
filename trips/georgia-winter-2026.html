Day-by-Day Itinerary
8 days / 7 nights — 6 nights Tbilisi, 1 night Gudauri. NYE in the city, one big ski day after the rush clears. Tap any day to expand it; tap 📍 on a stop to open it in Maps.
⚠️ Standby note: Dec 31–Jan 2 has high load factors on nearly every route. Plan A is Turkish via Istanbul (PHX → ORD or DFW → IST → TBS), and those legs are daily in both directions. Leaving PHX Dec 26 lands you in Tbilisi on Dec 28 — 01:35 or 10:05 depending on the Istanbul connection — so Day 1 below starts with a short night either way. Leave Dec 25 if you want a full recovery day first. See Getting There for the timetables.
Why the trip is shaped this way: Jan 1 is a dead day in Tbilisi — the country is home with family and most things are shut — so it's built around the two sights that don't care: an open-air monument with no opening hours, and a bar courtyard. The mountains are a single overnight: up on the afternoon of the 2nd with the UNESCO sites as stops en route, one full ski day on the 3rd, back down that evening. That leaves a relaxed final morning for the 11:05 flight rather than a pre-dawn scramble.

Mon · Dec 28
Arrival + First Taste of Tbilisi
Culture
MorningLand at TBS around dawn, Bolt into Old Town, drop bags and get horizontal for a few hours.
AfternoonPrivate room at the Abanotubani sulfur baths, plus a Kisa scrub to reset the travel day.
EveningEasy first dinner on Shardeni Street — khachapuri, wine, live music in the lanes.
Land, drop bags, get into the sulfur baths, and let Old Town do the rest. Don't fight the jet lag — lean into the evening. Everything today is within walking distance of the Riverview, which is the whole point of staying there.
Tbilisi International Airport (TBS) → Old Town📍 Map ~17km east of the center, roughly 25–30 min. Order a Bolt from inside the terminal — walk straight past the curbside taxi touts, who quote 3–4× the app price. The red-line metro also runs to the center for under 1 GEL if you're travelling light. Note: arrival time swings a lot by routing — a Turkish connection out of IST can land anywhere from early evening to ~01:35, while the BA fallback lands ~05:30. Message the hotel ahead about early check-in or a bag hold, and expect this day to flex around whatever you actually clear. 25–35 GEL (~$9–13)
Chreli Abano — Abanotubani Sulfur Baths📍 Map The domed brick bathhouse district below Narikala, fed by natural hot sulfur springs — the reason the city exists (Tbilisi means "warm place"). Book a private room rather than the public hall; you get your own pool, sauna, and marble slab for an hour. Ask for the Kisa — a full-body scrub-and-soap treatment by an attendant that is not gentle and is exactly what a 20-hour travel day needs. Some rooms have rooftop pools with fortress views. 60–80 GEL/room (~$22–30) · Kisa +15–25 GEL
Shardeni Street & Erekle II📍 Map Pedestrianized lanes in the heart of Old Town, packed with wine bars, cocktail bars, and restaurants with live music spilling into the street. More tourist-facing than Sololaki or Vera, but the festive-season atmosphere is genuinely electric and it's the right low-effort first night. Order Adjaruli khachapuri — the boat-shaped one with the egg cracked in at the table. Dinner 30–60 GEL (~$11–22) · wine 8–15 GEL/glass

Tue · Dec 29
Old Town, the Fortress & First Live Music
Culture Nightlife
MorningAnchiskhati and Sioni churches while they're still empty — the oldest rooms in the city.
AfternoonCable car up to Narikala Fortress, walk the ridge to the Mother Georgia statue.
EveningMulled Saperavi at the Christmas Village, then live jazz at Cafe Singer.
The classic Tbilisi day — start in the quiet churches, climb above the city, then hit the Christmas market at dusk and ease into the music scene at its most relaxed. The city is fully decorated: the festive season runs Dec 12 through Jan 14.
Sioni Cathedral + Anchiskhati Basilica📍 Map Two of the city's most important churches, five minutes apart, and worth doing first thing — they're near-empty before about 10 AM and the atmosphere is completely different for it. Anchiskhati (6th century) is the oldest surviving church in Tbilisi: small, dark, and much more affecting than its size suggests. Sioni holds the grapevine cross of St. Nino, who converted Georgia in the 4th century. Shoulders and knees covered; women should carry a scarf. Free · donations welcome
Narikala Fortress + Rike Park cable car📍 Map 4th-century fortress on the ridge over Old Town. Take the cable car up from Rike Park — it's a 3-minute ride with the best view in the city, and it saves you a steep cobblestone climb. The fortress itself is free and open; walk the walls, then continue along the ridge to the aluminium Mother Georgia statue (sword in one hand for enemies, wine bowl in the other for guests — the whole national character in one sculpture). 2.5 GEL each way (~$1) · fortress free
First Republic Square — Christmas Village📍 Map Runs Dec 12–Jan 14. Market stalls, live music most nights, and mulled Saperavi — hot spiced Georgian red, sold for pocket change. This is also where the NYE Gala Concert happens on the 31st, so it's worth a scout now to see how the square is laid out. Free entry · mulled wine 2–5 GEL
🎷 Jazz Cafe Singer📍 Map Live jazz and blues in a restored historic building in Old Town. Cozy, low-key, and the gentlest possible entry into Tbilisi's music scene — no face control, no dress code, no attitude. Just Georgian wine and a good band. Music generally starts around 8 PM and it's best on weekends. Usually no cover · drinks 10–20 GEL
Day 2 · per person~60–110 GEL (~$22–41)

Wed · Dec 30
Wine, Market Food & the Sololaki Crawl
Food & Wine Nightlife
MorningSecond bath session at Orbeliani — the one with the blue tiled façade.
AfternoonDezerter Bazaar for churchkhela, spices, cheese and general market chaos. Cash only.
EveningSololaki cellar wine crawl → live music at Mtkvarze → late-night khinkali run.
The best day of the trip if you like eating and drinking. Slow morning in the baths, chaotic food market at midday, then a cellar-to-cellar wine crawl that rolls into live music on the river and ends with the late-night khinkali run.
Orbeliani Baths📍 Map The one with the blue-and-turquoise tiled façade — the most photographed building in Abanotubani, done in Persian style. A second bath session sounds excessive until you've had one; it's the standard local hangover-and-life-reset move. Morning slot, before the market. 60–80 GEL/room (~$22–30)
Dezerter Bazaar📍 Map Tbilisi's sprawling, half-covered central market next to the train station — spice pyramids, wheels of sulguni cheese, dried fruit, walnuts, cured meat, and strings of churchkhela hanging like candles (walnuts threaded on string, dipped repeatedly in thickened grape juice — Georgia's original energy bar). Buy churchkhela here rather than the tourist shops in Old Town; it's a third of the price and fresher. Cash only. Churchkhela 3–8 GEL · browse budget 20–40 GEL
🍷 Vino Underground + Sololaki cellar crawl📍 Map Sololaki is the 19th-century Art Nouveau quarter below Narikala — crumbling grand facades, carved wooden doorways, and brick cellars converted into wine bars. Vino Underground is the anchor: a candlelit basement with one of the country's best natural-wine lists, all qvevri (fermented underground in clay vessels — an 8,000-year-old method). Order an amber wine — white grapes fermented on the skins, which is Georgia's actual signature style, not the reds. Then wander; the side streets off Gorgasali Square hide half a dozen more. 8–15 GEL/glass · degustation flight 30–60 GEL
🎸 Mtkvarze — live music on the riverbank📍 Map A converted Soviet-era restaurant building on the bank of the Mtkvari, now the city's best mixed-genre live venue — Georgian folk, jazz, occasional rock. Local crowd, no tourist markup, and the setting is the selling point: Metekhi Church floodlit on the cliff above, river below, Old Town on both sides. Doors around 8 PM. Cover 0–20 GEL · drinks 10–20 GEL
🥟 Late-night khinkali run📍 Map The canonical end to a Tbilisi night. Fat pleated dumplings full of spiced meat broth — hold by the topknot, bite a small hole, drink the broth first, then eat the rest and leave the doughy knot on your plate. The knots are how you count what you've eaten. Never use a fork; Georgians will notice. The specialists around Marjanishvili and near Fabrika stay open to 2–3 AM specifically for this. Order 5–7 per person. ~5 GEL each · 25–40 GEL/person
Day 3 · per person~150–250 GEL (~$56–93)

Thu · Dec 31
New Year's Eve in Tbilisi 🎆
Major Event
MorningSleep in. Shops shut early today — grab anything you need before mid-afternoon.
EveningThe supra — a full Georgian feast with a Tamada running toasts. Book weeks ahead.
MidnightCitywide fireworks from the ridge, then Bassiani or Khidi until dawn, then khinkali.
New Year's Eve is the holiday in Georgia — considerably bigger than Christmas. Families run enormous supras, the whole city sets off fireworks simultaneously at midnight (not a single organized display — thousands of them, from every balcony and courtyard), and the celebration rolls on for days.
⚠️ Traditional Supra Feast — BOOK 3–4 WEEKS AHEAD A supra is the formal Georgian feast: a table loaded end-to-end with khachapuri, khinkali, grilled meats, walnut-sauced vegetables, and endless wine, run by a Tamada (toastmaster) who delivers structured toasts all night and expects everyone to drink to each one. NYE set menus at good restaurants sell out weeks in advance — this is the single most important booking of the trip, more so than the hotel. 100–250 GEL/person (~$37–93) · NYE set menu
First Republic Square — Gala Concert + midnight fireworks📍 Map The main public celebration: big Georgian pop acts, countdown, and the citywide fireworks at midnight. Get somewhere with a view — the Narikala ridge and the bridges over the Mtkvari are spectacular, because you can see the entire city going off at once. Metro, buses, and the Rike ropeway run free until 3 AM. Free · transit free until 3 AM
🎧 After midnight: Bassiani or Khidi (optional)📍 Map NYE is one of Bassiani's biggest nights of the year. It's in the drained Olympic swimming pool beneath Dinamo Arena and is spoken of alongside Berghain. Register at bassiani.com several days in advance — walk-ups get turned away. Dress dark and plain, act sober in the queue, and expect a sticker over your phone camera at the door (no photos, no exceptions). Doors at midnight, runs to 10–11 AM; arrive after 2–3 AM to skip the line. Khidi, under the Vakhushti Bagrationi bridge, has similar music and an easier door — the better backup if Bassiani is slammed. Entry 20–40 GEL (~$7–15)
🥟 Post-club khinkali, 3–5 AM📍 Map Mandatory. Marjanishvili, near Fabrika. You'll know you've found the right place when half the tables are people still in club clothes. 25–40 GEL (~$9–15)

Fri · Jan 1
New Year's Day — Recovery + the Soviet Leftovers
Culture
MorningSleep. The entire country is at home with family and nothing is open.
AfternoonChronicle of Georgia — sixteen 30m monoliths on a hill, free, no opening hours to be closed.
EveningFabrika's courtyard — the one place reliably alive when the rest of the city is dark.
New Year's Day in Georgia is a national write-off — shops shut, restaurants closed, everyone recovering at home. That rules out most sightseeing, but not these two, which is exactly why they're here: the Chronicle is an unfenced open-air monument with no ticket office and no hours, and Fabrika is a bar courtyard, which is the category of place that stays open when everything else doesn't.
Dry Bridge Market is deliberately not today. It's an open-air flea market run by individual sellers, and on Jan 1 in Georgia it will not be happening. It's on Day 6 instead — a Saturday, which is when it's fullest anyway.
Chronicle of Georgia📍 Map Sixteen black steel-and-stone monoliths, 30m tall, carved with Georgian kings down one face and scenes from the life of Christ down the other, standing on a hill above the Tbilisi Sea reservoir. Unfinished, unfenced, and almost never crowded — the most staggering thing in the city that isn't in any of the guidebook top-tens. Go for the late-afternoon light; the stone takes it beautifully. About 30 minutes out by Bolt — have the driver wait, because you will not find a ride back from an empty hilltop on New Year's Day. Free · Bolt ~15–20 GEL each way — have it wait
Fabrika📍 Map A Soviet sewing factory in Marjanishvili converted into a hostel, bar, and studio complex, built around a big graffitied courtyard that functions as the social center of young Tbilisi. The courtyard is the point — you don't need to be staying there. Most likely thing to be open and busy tonight, and it's a short walk from the late-night khinkali places if the evening runs on. Free entry · drinks 10–20 GEL
⚠️ Plan your food today Restaurants that open at all on Jan 1 do so late and on reduced menus. Either ask the hotel what's running, or buy enough at Dezerter Bazaar on Day 3 — bread, cheese, churchkhela, cured meat — to not care. This is the one day of the trip where turning up hungry with no plan doesn't work. Supplies 20–40 GEL if self-catering
Day 5 · per person~50–90 GEL (~$19–33)

Sat · Jan 2
Bedoba — Dry Bridge, Mtskheta, then up to Gudauri
UNESCO Sites Market
MorningDry Bridge Market — medals, cameras, samovars, propaganda posters. Weekend is peak.
AfternoonCheck out and drive north: Svetitskhoveli and Jvari, the two UNESCO sites at Mtskheta.
EveningAnanuri on the reservoir in the last light, then up into Gudauri for the night.
Bedoba — the "day of luck," when Georgians hold that however you spend the day sets the tone for your whole year. It's also the fullest day on this itinerary, so start early. Market in the morning, then a midday departure north with the sights as stops rather than detours — Mtskheta, Jvari, and Ananuri all sit on or just off the Military Highway.
🚗 This is one GoTrip booking, not four separate rides. You book a single car for the whole route — Tbilisi → Mtskheta → Jvari → Ananuri → Gudauri — and stops and waiting time are included in the fixed price. The driver parks and waits at each site while you get out and look around, then drives on. These are real visits, not things you glimpse through a window. Specify all three stops when you book so the route is priced correctly.

Don't try this with Bolt: it won't wait 45 minutes at Jvari, and you will not find a car to pick you up at Ananuri.

The driver is not a guide — GoTrip drivers drive, and their English is often limited. If you want the Svetitskhoveli guide, that's hired separately at the cathedral.
⏱ Leave Tbilisi by about 12:30. Sunset in Georgia in early January is around 17:30, and Ananuri on a frozen reservoir is worth seeing in daylight. If you're running behind, drop Svetitskhoveli and keep Jvari — the clifftop view over the two rivers is the part you'll remember.
Dry Bridge Market📍 Map An open-air flea market where people sell off the Soviet Union — medals, film cameras, samovars, porcelain, propaganda posters, sheet music, chandeliers, occasionally something genuinely old. Half junk, half remarkable, and the best souvenir stop in the country by a distance. Haggling is expected and stays friendly. Busiest at weekends, so a Saturday morning is the right slot. Bring cash and give it 1–2 hours. Free to browse · buy what you like
Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, Mtskheta📍 Map Twenty minutes out of Tbilisi in the ancient capital. UNESCO-listed, 11th century, and the holiest church in Georgia — tradition holds Christ's robe is buried beneath it. Vast interior, surviving frescoes. A guide genuinely changes this stop: without one it's handsome stonework, with one it's the founding story of Georgian Christianity. Worth the money here more than anywhere else on the trip. Free entry · guide ~90–120 GEL (~$33–44) for the group
Jvari Monastery📍 Map 590–605 AD, UNESCO-listed, on a bluff directly above where the Aragvi and Mtkvari rivers meet — you can see the two running side by side in different colours before they merge. Ten minutes' drive above Mtskheta and the best viewpoint of the day. The wind up there in winter is serious; don't linger in a thin jacket. Free
Ananuri Fortress📍 Map A 17th-century fortified church complex on the Zhinvali reservoir, directly on the highway. Two churches inside the curtain wall, one with vivid surviving frescoes and a carved grapevine cross on the façade. Free, about 45 minutes, and the reservoir in low winter light is worth the stop by itself. Aim to be here by ~16:00. Free
Arrive Gudauri — check in📍 Map Into the resort around dusk. Sort your gear rental tonight if any shop is still open — it buys you the first lift tomorrow instead of standing in a queue at 10 AM, and tomorrow is the only ski day. One drink, then sleep. Hit an ATM in Tbilisi before you leave; smaller Gudauri restaurants and rental shops are cash-only. GoTrip ~$60/car (~160 GEL, split) — covers the whole day's route
Day 6 · per person~80–140 GEL + car share (~$30–52)

Sun · Jan 3
The Ski Day — Gudauri, then Back Down
Skiing
MorningFirst lift at 10. This is the only ski day, so take all of it.
AfternoonKobi–Gudauri gondola for the Kazbek panorama; optional tandem paraglide off the ridge.
EveningReturn gear, drive back down to Tbilisi, in around 20:00–20:30.
One full day on the hill, first lift to last, then the drive back down. Because it's the only ski day, there's no case for a lazy start — you're paying the same gear rental whether you ride four hours or seven. Lifts run 10:00–17:00.
Gudauri Ski Resort📍 Map 35km of groomed piste topping out at 3,276m, plus the off-piste and freeride terrain that gets the Caucasus mentioned alongside Japan and BC. Snow depth typically 1–2m. Almost entirely above the treeline, so it's wide-open bowls rather than cut runs, on modern lifts. Northern slopes hold the best snow. Take the Kobi–Gudauri gondola at least once for the Kazbek panorama — on one ski day it's the thing not to skip. Day pass 50–70 GEL (~$18–26)
Gear rental Skis or board, boots, poles, helmet, from the shops at the base. Bring your own helmet if you have one. Goggles and SPF 30+ are non-negotiable at this altitude even under cloud. Sort this the night before if you can. 40–60 GEL/day (~$15–22)
On-mountain food & drinks Mountain restaurants and base-area bars. Keep the après short today — there's a mountain descent afterwards and you want the driver's full attention, not a delayed departure. 50–80 GEL (~$18–30)
🪂 Optional: tandem paragliding📍 Map Launch off the ridge and fly the Caucasus with the valley underneath, 10–20 minutes airborne. Weather-dependent and cancels readily on winter wind. Worth thinking twice about on a single ski day — booking, waiting, and flying can eat two hours of slope time you don't have spare. ~299 GEL (~$111) · optional
⚠️ The drive back down, after dark🔗 GeoRoad Gear back by 17:15, on the road by about 18:00, into Tbilisi around 20:00–20:30. This is the one real compromise in the plan. The Tbilisi–Gudauri section is well maintained and GoTrip drivers run it constantly, and the closures that do happen are on the stretch north toward Kazbegi — but a winter mountain descent after dark is genuinely more exposed than the same road at noon: black ice, poor visibility, and fresh snow can outpace the plows. Check GeoRoad or call +995 322 313 076 before you commit, and confirm the driver that morning.

Built-in fallback: if the forecast turns, stay in Gudauri and drive down early on the 4th instead. The 11:05 flight is late enough to absorb it — leave Gudauri by 06:00 and you're at TBS around 09:00 with time to spare. That flexibility is a direct benefit of the Istanbul routing; the old 07:30 BA departure would not have allowed it. GoTrip car ~$60 total (~160 GEL, split)

PHX → TBS on Turkish via Istanbul. Timetables below; all times are local to the airport, +1 marks a next-day arrival.

Fallback Routings

Alternate — BA via London (oneworld)
PHX → Gateway → LHR → TBS
AA metal to LHR, then BA890 — departs 21:25, lands Tbilisi 05:30+1. Only flies Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun, so rolling off it can cost two or three days. Return is BA891 at 07:30 most mornings, which forces a ~05:30 airport arrival on departure day.
Alternate — Qatar via Doha (oneworld)
PHX → Gateway → DOH → TBS
The cleanest option if the AA↔TK ZED agreement doesn't pan out, since Qatar is oneworld. DOH–TBS runs Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat — better than BA's four days, still not daily, and a substantial detour south. Bench depth rather than a real contender.
Alliances don't govern non-rev — ZED agreements do. Turkish being Star Alliance rather than oneworld doesn't by itself put it out of reach: employee standby runs on bilateral ZED agreements between individual carriers, which cross alliance lines freely. Confirm the AA↔TK agreement and the applicable ZED fare level with AA HR before building the trip around it.

Connecting at Istanbul

IST is enormous — one of the largest terminals in the world, and transfers between Turkish flights are airside, so there's no immigration queue on a same-day connection. Allow more walking time than the signage suggests. On the 7h25m ORD layover you can clear landside into the city, but budget 3 hrs round trip and remember you'd be re-clearing security. Turkish operates a transit hotel inside the terminal, and longer layovers may qualify for their complimentary TourIstanbul tours — ask at the transfer desk rather than assuming.
Verify before booking. These are current published timetables and airlines reshuffle seasonally — re-check every time above closer to departure, especially the IST→TBS departures, which are the load-bearing part of this plan.

Airport Info

Tbilisi International Airport (TBS) — Shota Rustaveli Airport. Located ~17km east of the city center. Bolt from airport to Old Town: 25–35 GEL (~$9–13 USD). Metro (red line) also connects the airport to the city center cheaply. Avoid curbside taxi touts.
Getting Around Georgia
No unified national network — you mix tools depending on the leg. Affordable once you know the system.

Within Tbilisi

🚇 Metro
Two lines, efficient, under 1 GEL/ride. Runs free until 3 AM on New Year's Eve. Best for cross-city trips.
📱 Bolt
Go-to ride app. Like Uber. Upfront pricing, Apple Pay. No language barrier. Always use app over street taxis.

Intercity

🚗 GoTrip.ge
A hired car with a driver for a whole route, not a point-to-point taxi. Unlimited stops and waiting time are included in one fixed price — the driver parks and waits while you go look at things. English interface, price locked at booking, pay at the end. Book in advance.
🚐 Marshrutka
Minibus network. Cheapest option (5–25 GEL). Leave when full — no fixed schedule. Cash only to driver. Depart from Didube (north/west) or Ortachala (east).
Key distinction: Didube and Ortachala bus stations are absolutely nowhere near each other — confirm which station serves your route before leaving your hotel.

Gudauri
Greater Caucasus · 2 hrs from Tbilisi
Recommended
Best infrastructure, easiest access, most terrain. 35km of groomed slopes plus extensive off-piste and freeride that gets the Caucasus mentioned in the same breath as Japan and British Columbia. Home to the highest-altitude adventure tourism school in Europe (IFMGA certified). Snow depth averages 1–2m during the season. Lifts open 10am–5pm. Cash still king for smaller restaurants and rental shops — bring GEL from Tbilisi ATMs.

⚠️ Confirm the 2026–27 opening date before booking — Gudauri typically opens in late December, but the exact date shifts with snowfall each year and early-season cover can be thin. Jan–Feb are peak conditions; your Jan 3 ski day sits right at the start of the good window.

Road to Gudauri — Safety

Safe with a driver, more nuanced if self-driving. The Military Highway (Tbilisi → Gudauri) is well-maintained and regularly cleared. Winter tires are mandatory. Road closures happen on the section north of Gudauri (Gudauri → Kazbegi), not between Tbilisi and Gudauri. GoTrip drivers are vetted and equipped. Check GeoRoad Facebook Page or call +995 322 313 076 for real-time road status.

Alternative Activities
Swap-ins if the plan changes — weather closes a road, the ski day gets rained out, or you just want something different. Ordered by how easily each one drops into the existing schedule.

Swap Into a Tbilisi Day

Mtatsminda Park + funicular📍 Map
Funicular ~5–10 GEL · 2–3 hrs
The funicular climbs from the city to a ridgetop amusement park at 770m with a Ferris wheel and the widest view over Tbilisi. Faintly surreal in winter, mostly empty, and the ride itself is the attraction. The Pantheon of Writers — where much of Georgian literary and political history is buried — is at the mid-station.
Georgian National Museum📍 Map
~15–25 GEL · Rustaveli Ave · 1.5–2 hrs
Go for two things: the Treasury of pre-Christian Colchian goldwork (2nd millennium BC, and extraordinary), and the Museum of Soviet Occupation upstairs, which is a bracing corrective to any romantic notions about the period. A good rainy-day or hangover option since it's indoors and central.

Day Trips Out of the City

Kakheti wine region — Sighnaghi, Bodbe, Telavi📍 Map
Group tour ~$13–39pp · private from ~$99 · full day, ~2 hrs each way
The obvious big swap. Kakheti produces most of Georgia's wine, and Sighnaghi is a walled hilltop town overlooking the Alazani Valley with the Caucasus behind it. Winter means bare vines but also empty tasting rooms and full attention from the winemakers. Tasting costs vary sharply by venue: family maranis are often free or 10–15 GEL with snacks, large factory tastings 15–25 GEL, boutique producers 30–60 GEL. Add Bodbe Monastery (St. Nino's burial place) which is usually bundled in.
⚠️ Kazbegi / Stepantsminda + Gergeti Trinity📍 Map
Group ~$13–39pp · 4x4 up to church ~20 GEL · full day, ~3 hrs each way
The postcard shot of Georgia: a 14th-century stone church alone on a ridge at 2,170m with the 5,054m glaciated cone of Mt. Kazbek behind it. The winter caveat is real — the road closures on the Military Highway hit exactly the stretch north of Gudauri toward Kazbegi, so this trip can be cancelled at short notice by snow. With only one Gudauri night (Jan 2) and one ski day, this no longer slots in easily — it would mean giving up the ski day entirely, or adding a second Gudauri night. Treat it as a straight swap for skiing rather than an addition, and only on a clear forecast; check GeoRoad first. The final climb to the church needs a 4x4 in winter; don't plan to walk it.
Uplistsikhe cave city + Gori📍 Map
Entries ~15–25 GEL each · full day, ~1.5 hrs each way
A rock-hewn town carved into a cliff face, inhabited from roughly the 1st millennium BC — halls, wine cellars, a theatre, and a pagan temple all cut from living stone. Pairs with Gori, Stalin's birthplace, where the museum is a genuinely strange experience: still largely presented as it was in Soviet times, with his preserved birth house and personal railway carriage. Uncomfortable and worth seeing.
Borjomi📍 Map
Park entry ~2–7 GEL · full day, ~2 hrs each way
The mineral-water spa town, in a forested gorge in the Lesser Caucasus. Faded imperial-resort architecture, a central park where you drink the famously sulfurous water straight from the spring, and open-air warm pools. A gentler day than the mountains — good if someone in the group wants a rest day that isn't just sitting in Tbilisi.

Food & Wine Experiences

Georgian cooking class
~80–150 GEL/person · 3–4 hrs
Usually khachapuri and khinkali, occasionally churchkhela. The khinkali pleating is much harder than it looks and the class is mostly an excuse to eat what you made with a lot of wine. Widely offered in Old Town; several run out of family homes, which is the better version. A strong rainy-day or road-closed substitute.
In-city qvevri tasting / wine cellar degustation
30–60 GEL/person · 1–2 hrs
If Kakheti won't fit, you can get most of the education in Tbilisi. A guided degustation walks you through amber/skin-contact whites (Rkatsiteli, Mtsvane) against the signature red (Saperavi), and explains the qvevri method properly. Sololaki and Vera are full of these — it's a deeper version of what Day 3 already does casually.
Sulfur bath, again
60–80 GEL/room · 1 hr
Listed as an alternative because it's the default local answer to almost any question — too cold, too hungover, too tired, plans fell through. Two sessions are already in the itinerary; a third is not excessive by Tbilisi standards.

Winter & Active

Tandem paragliding over Gudauri📍 Map
~299 GEL/person · 10–20 min flight
Launch off the ridge and fly the Caucasus with the valley underneath. One of the most accessible tandem operations anywhere and cheap by alpine standards. Weather-dependent and cancels readily on winter wind, so book the day before and hold it loosely. Already flagged as optional on Day 6.
Heli-skiing from Gudauri
Price on enquiry · full day
Gudauri runs one of the world's more affordable heli operations — a fraction of Alaska or BC pricing for comparable Caucasus terrain. Not a casual add: it needs a booking, avalanche kit, and confident off-piste skiing. Enquire at the resort the evening before, or through a local operator in advance if it's a priority.
Snowmobiling & snowshoeing, Gudauri
Varies · 1–3 hrs
The fallback if the lifts shut on wind — which happens at Gudauri, since it's mostly above the treeline and exposed. Both are bookable at the base area on the day. Useful to know about before you find yourself with a paid lift pass and closed lifts.

If the Trip Ever Extends

Jan 7 — Orthodox Christmas & the Alilo procession📍 Map
Free · ~2 PM, First Republic Square → Sameba Cathedral
Georgia keeps the Julian calendar, so Christmas falls on Jan 7. Thousands process through Old Tbilisi in costume, chanting, carrying banners, collecting alms for the poor, ending at the enormous Sameba Cathedral. It is the single most striking public event of the Georgian year and a Jan 4 departure misses it by three days. If the standby flights ever make a longer trip viable, this is the reason.
Mestia & Svaneti📍 Map
8–10 hrs from Tbilisi · needs 3+ days
Medieval stone defensive towers scattered through high Caucasus villages, plus Hatsvali and Tetnuldi ski areas and world-class backcountry. Too far to bolt onto this trip — it's the reason for a second one. Flights from Tbilisi to Mestia exist and cut the journey dramatically, but they're small aircraft and weather-cancel often.
Batumi📍 Map
5–6 hrs by train/road · needs 2+ days
Black Sea coast — subtropical, palm trees, a boulevard of aggressively strange modern architecture, and a casino scene. Very quiet out of season, which some people prefer. The overnight or fast train from Tbilisi is comfortable and cheap. Off-plan for a winter trip built around snow, but worth knowing it exists.

Everything you need to know before you go.

Holidays & Calendar

🎄
Dec 25 — Western Christmas
Celebrated but subdued. Christmas Village at First Republic Square is already in full swing (runs Dec 12–Jan 14). Mulled wine, markets, live music nightly.
🎆
Dec 31 — New Year's Eve (Biggest night of the year)
NYE is bigger than Christmas in Georgia. Elaborate family supras (feasts), citywide fireworks at midnight, free public transport until 3 AM. Book restaurants 3–4 weeks ahead minimum. Christmas Village hosts the main Gala Concert.
🍀
Jan 2 — Bedoba (Day of Luck)
How you spend this day is said to define your whole year. Georgians keep it calm — brunch, spa, relaxed gatherings. Perfect excuse to do a morning bath session before heading to the mountains.
⛪
Jan 7 — Orthodox Christmas + Alilo Procession
Georgia uses the Julian calendar — Christmas falls on Jan 7. The Alilo Procession starts at ~2pm from First Republic Square, ending at Sameba Cathedral. Thousands chanting through Old Tbilisi's cobblestone streets. One of Georgia's most remarkable public events. Consider extending the trip to catch it.

Money & Payments

💳
Currency: Georgian Lari (GEL)
1 USD ≈ 2.7 GEL. Cards widely accepted in Tbilisi hotels, restaurants, and Gudauri lift ticket offices. Smaller mountain restaurants and marshrutka drivers are cash only. Withdraw GEL from Tbilisi ATMs before heading to the mountains. Bring backup cash from PHX.
🚕
Never take a curbside taxi
Unmetered, tourist pricing is common. Always use Bolt in cities. Airport curbside taxi touts are especially aggressive — walk past them to Bolt pickup zones or metro.

Food & Drink Must-Tries

🧀
Khachapuri
Georgia's iconic cheese bread. The Adjarian variety (Adjaruli) is a boat-shaped bread loaded with melted cheese, butter, and a raw egg cracked in at the table. Non-negotiable order.
🥟
Khinkali
Georgian soup dumplings. Hold by the knob, bite a small hole, suck the broth, then eat the rest. Do not eat the knob — it's a handle, not food. Pasanauri town on the drive to Gudauri is famous for its khinkali; stop for lunch.
🍷
Qvevri Wine
Georgia has 8,000 years of winemaking history. Qvevri wines are fermented underground in large clay vessels — especially the amber/orange wines made from white grapes with skin contact. Rkatsiteli and Mtsvane are the classic Georgian white varieties; Saperavi is the signature red. Always order a degustation in a Tbilisi wine cellar.
🌰
Churchkhela
Georgia's "Snickers" — walnuts or hazelnuts threaded on a string and dipped in thickened grape juice. Found at every market. Buy from Dezerter Bazaar, not tourist shops.

Packing for Winter

🧥
Tbilisi: Chilly European winter
December averages 7°C (45°F) highs, dropping to -2°C (28°F) overnight. January is colder: ~4°C (39°F) highs. Pack a real winter coat, warm layers, and waterproof shoes for cobblestone streets. About 51% clear/sunny days in December — don't expect constant gray.
⛷️
Gudauri: Full mountain kit
Ski gear rentable on-site (40–60 GEL/day). Bring your own helmet if you have one — rental helmets at mountain resorts worldwide are hit or miss. Sunglasses and SPF 30+ essential at altitude even in winter. Layers: resort base area is warmer than on-piste.
👗
Modest dress for religious sites
Shoulders and knees covered for Mtskheta, Ananuri, and any churches. Women should have a scarf for head covering inside active churches. This applies to Jvari Monastery, Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, and Gergeti Trinity if you add Kazbegi.

Nightlife

🍷
Wine Bars — Sololaki & Vera (Start Here)
The best casual nightlife in Tbilisi. Underground brick cellars serving natural qvevri wines by the glass (8–15 GEL). Vino Underground in Sololaki is the anchor — one of Georgia's best natural wine lists in a candlelit cellar. Side streets off Gorgasali Square have additional gems. The Vera district is more local and slightly less polished — both worth exploring. Bars open ~7 PM and run late.
🎷
Jazz Cafe Singer — Live Jazz in Old Town
Live jazz and blues in a beautifully restored historic building. Cozy, soulful, no agenda. The most relaxed music venue in the city — no face control, no dress code, just Georgian wine and good music. Best on weekends. Open from ~8 PM.
🎸
Mtkvarze — Live Folk, Jazz & Rock on the River
Tbilisi's best live music venue for mixed genres — Georgian folk acts, jazz, and rock on the banks of the Mtkvari. Informal and local without tourist-bar pricing. The setting is remarkable: Metekhi Church lit up on the cliff above, Old Town on both sides. Open ~8 PM.
🎧
Bassiani — The Techno Club (Optional, Commitment Required)
Ranked alongside Berghain as one of the world's great techno clubs. Housed in the former Olympic swimming pool under Dinamo Arena stadium — a massive concrete space with an immersive sound system. Fri & Sat only, midnight to 10–11 AM.

Rules: Register at bassiani.com a few days before — don't just show up. Dress dark and unpretentious (black hoodie, casual). Camera taped over at the door — no photos, no exceptions. Act sober in line. Arrive after 3 AM to skip the queue. Entry 20–40 GEL.

It's not for everyone — it's pure techno, 10+ hours, very serious about the music. But if you want to see why Tbilisi has a global reputation, this is it.
🌉
Khidi — Under the Bridge
Occupies the underside of the Vakhushti Bagrationi Bridge over the Mtkvari, with outdoor decks and a harder industrial aesthetic. Similar electronic music programming to Bassiani with a slightly less intimidating door. Good backup if Bassiani is too packed on NYE.
🎨
Café Gallery — Most Accessible Club
Art space meets electronic music in Old Town. Similar vibes to Bassiani but more accessible door policy — a good first-timer entry point into Tbilisi's club scene. Good for NYE if Bassiani feels like too much of a commitment.
🥟
🌟 MUST: Late-Night Khinkali Run
The canonical post-bar, post-club food in Tbilisi. Late-night khinkali specialists near Fabrika and in the Marjanishvili area are open until 2–3 AM specifically to feed people coming off a long night. How to eat them: Hold by the topknot, bite a small hole, drink the broth first, then eat the dumpling. Leave the doughy knot on the plate — it's a counter of how many you've had. Do not use a fork. Georgians will notice.
💰
Nightlife Budget
A full evening — dinner, wine bar, and club — runs 80–150 GEL/person ($29–55 USD). Wine by the glass: 8–15 GEL. Craft beer: 8–12 GEL. Cocktails: 15–25 GEL. Club entry (Bassiani/Khidi): 20–40 GEL. Late-night khinkali: ~5 GEL each. One of the best-value nightlife cities in Europe.

Accommodation

🏨
Hilton Garden Inn Tbilisi Riverview ✅
Ranked #6 of 498 hotels in Tbilisi (4.7★). Located near Old Town, within walking distance of Narikala Fortress, Metekhi Church, and the sulfur baths. 10 minutes from the historical center, 15 minutes from TBS airport. Mom's friend & family discount: ~30–50% off (~$50–80/night est.).

⚠️ Book immediately — NYE availability disappears fast. Confirm how the discount is applied; you may need to call the hotel directly rather than booking online.

Avoid: Hilton Garden Inn Tbilisi Chavchavadze — it's in the Vake district, further from Old Town, and requires a Bolt for every activity.
⛷️
Gudauri — Book Separately
The HGI deal covers Tbilisi nights only. For Gudauri (one night only, Jan 2), book directly at the resort. Budget 120–200 GEL/night (~$44–74). Gudauri Lodge is the best option — 4.3★, 5-star hotel with spa, pool, and sauna. Also consider Gudauri Hut (4.5★, cozy and family-friendly with free breakfast). Book early — January fills up after the NYE rush clears and ski season hits full stride.
